Mrs. Uprety (Nepal), Rapporteur of the Second
Committee: It is an honour for me to introduce to the
General Assembly the reports of the Second Committee
on the agenda items allocated to it by the General
Assembly at its seventy-sixth session.
The reports, contained in documents A/76/530 to
A/76/542, include the texts of draft resolutions and draft
decisions recommended by the Second Committee to
the General Assembly for adoption. For the convenience
of delegations, a checklist of actions taken in the
Committee has been prepared and circulated by the
Secretariat in document A/C.2/76/INF/1.
At its first meeting, on 1 October, taking into
account the situation regarding the coronavirus
disease (COVID-19) pandemic, the limitations on the
number of meetings held on United Nations premises
as a precautionary measure aimed at containing the
spread of COVID-19 and the available technological
and procedural solutions, and also bearing in mind the
conference room paper of the Bureau of the Second
Committee on the work of the Committee during the

seventy-sixth session of the General Assembly, the
Second Committee approved the working arrangements
of the Committee for the seventy-sixth session as set
out in document A/C.2/76/L.1, entitled “Organization
of the work of the Second Committee”.
As a result, during the main part of the seventysixth session of the General Assembly, the Second
Committee held 10 in-person plenary meetings to hold
its general debate, as well as to take action on the draft
proposals before it, and 12 virtual informal meetings
with remote simultaneous interpretation to hold
substantive discussions on individual items.
In addition, the Committee held a joint in-person
meeting with the Economic and Social Council,
as well as its annual dialogue with the Executive
Secretaries of the Regional Commissions, and one
side event, both using an online platform with remote
simultaneous interpretation.
The Second Committee adopted a total of 37 draft
resolutions, nine of which were adopted by recorded
vote, and two draft decisions on the revitalization of the
work of the Committee.

eradication of poverty and hunger by 2030. This issue
is of vital importance to my dear country, Haiti, and,
of course, to the majority of the inhabitants of this
planet, who on a daily basis live the sad reality of social
injustice, which is all too often incorrectly described
as underdevelopment.
There is no need to say that the eradication of poverty
and hunger in the world is a lofty goal, nor is there a
need to reiterate that the Republic of Haiti supports that
lofty goal. However, achieving that goal will necessarily
involve a different approach to development; in other
words, we need to rethink and redefine the concept of
development in order to reach that goal. We must stop
limiting the concept to a simple technical question of
economic growth or macroeconomic stability, although
those things are certainly important. Other inherent
attributes must also be included. The international
financial institutions must abandon the so-called
one-size-fits-all development strategy or theory, which
can only bog down the countries for which it was
conceived in the mire of poverty and hunger because,
quite simply and entirely logically, each country has its
own specificities that need to be taken into account in
any development process. In other words, every country
has its own development needs.
More significantly, the concept of development
must be considered as a process, the end result of which
is the deep-rooted social transformation of individuals,
particularly the most vulnerable. In the context of the
global economy or globalization in general, we should
promote and implement a development model that first
and foremost targets the socioeconomic development of
the poorest.
Today nothing can justify the continued existence
of poverty in the world, just as nothing could justify

human dignity.
