Reports of the First Committee
The President: The General Assembly will take up
the reports of the First Committee on agenda items 92
to 107, 122 and 139.
I now request the Rapporteur of the First Committee,
Ms. Sanna Orava of Finland, to introduce the reports of
the Committee in one intervention.
Ms. Orava (Finland), Rapporteur of the First
Committee: I have the honour to submit for the
consideration of the General Assembly the draft
resolutions and draft decisions recommended by the
Disarmament and International Security Committee,
the First Committee, in its reports on agenda items 92 to
107, 122 and 139, as contained in documents A/76/390
to A/76/436. For the convenience of delegations, a list
of the proposals contained in the reports of the First
Committee for consideration by the General Assembly
has been prepared by the Secretariat and issued as
document A/C.1/76/INF/3.
While convening a full-fledged session of the First
Committee for the main part of the seventy-sixth session
of the General Assembly was challenging owing to the
constraints of the coronavirus disease (COVID-19)
pandemic, the Committee made good progress in
formulating a programme of work that included all
three traditional segments — a general debate, thematic
discussions and an action phase. The Bureau of the
First Committee, ably chaired by Ambassador Omar
Hilale of Morocco, engaged in extensive consultations

with delegations prior to the session with the aim of
building on the success of previous years by proceeding
with the work of the Committee amid the pandemic,
all the while ensuring the participation of all Member
States on an equal footing. To that end, the Committee
adopted a number of temporary measures in the light
of the unique circumstances imposed by the COVID-19
pandemic. Time limits for statements were strictly
observed. A compendium of statements on the specific
thematic clusters sets out the positions of delegations
that could not be delivered in person. Statements in
explanation of vote and position and in the exercise of
the right of reply were substantially shortened.
Having convened 18 in-person meetings and three
virtual meetings, the seventy-sixth session of the First
Committee proceeded very smoothly and in doing so
achieved its goals of contributing meaningfully to the
General Assembly in the field of disarmament and
international security. After holding its organizational
meeting on 30 September (see A/C.1/76/PV.1), the First
Committee completed its general debate and thematic
discussions in record time. The general debate on the
16 substantive agenda items and 36 sub-items allocated
to the Committee was convened from 4 to 12 October.
We were honoured to have you, Mr. President, address
the Committee at its meeting on 13 October (see
A/C.1/76/PV.9). At this year’s session, 137 delegations
made statements during the general debate segment,
as compared to 143 in 2020. I should like to point out,
however, that only 33 of those statements were delivered
by women this year, which shows that we still have a
long way to go to achieve equal gender representation

During the action phase, the Committee adopted
60 draft resolutions and decisions, 37 of which were
adopted by recorded vote, with 66 separate votes
having been requested. The Secretariat made oral
statements on the financial implications of four draft
proposals, as reflected in document A/C.1/76/INF/3,
which is before the Assembly today. Six draft proposals
attracted statements by the Secretary-General on their
programme budget implications. In keeping with past
practice, the General Assembly will consider the five
adopted draft proposals with budget implications
after their consideration by the Fifth Committee. No
proposals were put forward on the agenda item entitled
“Reduction of military budgets”.
With regard to agenda item 122, “Revitalization of
the work of the General Assembly”, the draft decision
containing the provisional programme of work and
timetable of the First Committee for 2022 is contained
in document A/76/452. After two years of having
temporary COVID-19 measures in place, we hope that
2022 will see a return to a full-fledged Committee
session, which will allow for full in-person attendance,
including with the presence of members of civil society.
I would like to recall the particularly high level of
cooperation that prevailed in the First Committee this
year. The Committee was able to fulfil the mandate
entrusted to it by the Assembly. I should like to express,
on behalf of the Bureau of the Committee, our sincere
appreciation to all delegations for their constructive
participation and flexibility throughout the session.
Despite good cooperation, as I mentioned earlier, more
than 100 recorded votes were once again requested on
the 60 resolutions and decisions adopted by the First
Committee this year. That is a lot. It reduces the time
available for the substantive discussions and means
there is a lot of work for delegations in preparation for
the action phase, including relatively more work for
the smaller missions. In my view as Rapporteur, the
First Committee might benefit from making public
the requests for a vote, as is already the established
practice in the other Main Committees. That would
encourage finding consensus and thereby limit the
voting to only the most necessary proposals, and it
would certainly enhance the transparency of the work
of the First Committee.

we will proceed to take decisions in the same manner

the same. I should also hope that we may proceed to

under plenary announcements.

consideration by the General Assembly”, which has
been issued as document A/C.1/76/INF/3. Members
are reminded that additional co-sponsors are no longer
accepted now that the draft resolutions and decisions
have been adopted by the Committee. Any clarification
about co-sponsorship in the Committee reports should
be addressed to the Secretary of the Committee.
Furthermore, members are reminded that any
corrections to the voting intentions of delegations
after the voting has concluded on a proposal should be
addressed directly to the Secretariat after the meeting.
I count on members’ cooperation in avoiding any
interruptions to our proceedings in that regard.
