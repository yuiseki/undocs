The President: The General Assembly will take up
the reports of the First Committee on agenda items 87

I request the Rapporteur of the First Committee,

Mrs. Hassan (Djibouti), Rapporteur of the First
Committee: It gives me great pleasure to introduce to
the General Assembly the reports of the First Committee
on agenda items 87 to 104, 118 and 133. The reports are
contained in documents A/69/431 through A/69/450.
In response to the General Assembly’s appeal,
the First Committee continued to fully utilize its
allocated resources efficiently and was able to conclude
its work at the current session in four weeks, in full
accordance with the planned completion date, with 24
formal meetings. The work of the Committee at the
current session was very efficient, procedurally smooth
and substantively constructive, especially in the light
of the ongoing efforts to re-energize the process of
disarmament in multilateral forums.
Twenty-two agenda items were allocated to the
Committee, including a new sub-item, under the item
entitled “Prevention of an arms race in outer space”.
In the course of the general debate, 107 delegations,
including observers, made statements. Among those,
eight spoke on behalf of various groups. A recordbreaking total of 265 statements were made within

the framework of thematic discussions at the second
stage of the Committee’s work. Also during the second
stage, the Committee held exchanges with the High
Representative for Disarmament Affairs and other
high-level officials in the field of arms control and
disarmament, including from the International Atomic
Energy Agency, the Preparatory Commission for the
Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization,
the Organization for the Prohibition of Chemical
Weapons, the Conference on Disarmament and the
Disarmament Commission, among others, as well as
with civil society organizations.
Notably, the Committee’s high-level exchange,
held on 16 October, on the “Current state of affairs in
the field of arms control and disarmament and the role
of international organizations with mandates in this
field”, was aided by a sub-theme entitled “Increasing
capacities to address weapons of mass destruction”,
proposed by the Chair and by his concept paper thereon.
These made it possible for discussions on the item to be
much better focused.
During the third and final stage of its work,
devoted to action on draft resolutions and decisions,
the First Committee had before it 57 draft resolutions
and 6 decisions. Of the 63 adopted by the Committee,
32 drafts, or 51 per cent, were adopted without a vote.
Meeting on the heels of the ministerial-level
informal meeting of the plenary convened by you,
Mr. President, to commemorate the “International
Day for the Total Elimination of Nuclear Weapons” on
26 September, the First Committee saw this year that
