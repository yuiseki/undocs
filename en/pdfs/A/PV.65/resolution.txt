Mr. Wellington Koo (China) : Mr. President,
in reply to the question which you have been
good enough to put to me, I will recall to the
Assembly that a similar question arose at the first
part of the first session of the Assembly in Lon:don. There was then some doubt, and I expressed the view that, in accordance with our
rules of procedure, the number of delegations
which should be counted in the total number of
votes were the number of delegations who were
present and voting, as the rules of procedure
speak of "the majority of the representatives
present and voting." Therefore, those who voted,
although their ballots are for one reason or another to be considered invalid, would still be
counted in the total number as they are present
and have voted. Thus, it would be quite in accordance with the letter and the spirit of our
rules of procedure to say that the delegations
which voted, although they voted incorrectly and
therefore their votes should not count, should
still be counted among the total number on the
basis of which should decide whether there is a
majority.
