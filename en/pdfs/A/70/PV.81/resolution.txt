Ms. Engelbrecht Schadtler (Bolivarian Republic
of Venezuela) (spoke in Spanish): The Bolivarian
Republic of Venezuela welcomes the adoption of
resolution 70/185, entitled “Unilateral economic
measures as a means of political and economic coercion
against developing countries”, which was submitted by
the Group of 77 and China as part of the work of the
Second Committee.
Our country voted in favour of this resolution
because it believes that unilateral measures run counter
to the purposes and principles of the Charter of the
United Nations and the Declaration on the Principles
of International Law with respect to friendly relations
and cooperation among States Members of the United
Nations. Such measures are a threat to respect for
equal rights among States, the self-determination of
peoples and non-intervention in the internal affairs of
States, as well as the inalienable right of all States to
determine their own political, social, economic and
cultural system without interference from any other
State. Imposing unilateral measures adversely affects
the normal political, economic, social and cultural
development of the country being subjected to those
measures and violates the human rights of its nationals.
Unilateral measures have been imposed on our
country, and we categorically reject them. Bearing in
mind the spirit of the resolution just adopted, we hope
that the international community will support the
adoption of urgent and effective measures to address,
condemn and reject such illegal and illegitimate
measures as they are used as a way to exercise political
and economic pressure on our country.

The President: I now give the floor to the
representative of Argentina, who wishes to speak in
explanation of position.
Mr. Fernández Valoni (Argentina) (spoke in
Spanish): Argentina has joined the consensus on the
adoption of resolution 70/226 owing to the importance
it attaches to the conservation and sustainable use of
the oceans and seas and their resources. We also value
the constructive spirit with which the negotiations were
on the resolution were carried out. However, we would
like to put on record our position.
Argentina is a country that attaches top priority
to the oceans and their resources. Along with other
countries of Latin America, we were pioneers in the
development of the new law of the sea and concepts
that were later recognized by the United Nations
Convention on the Law of the Sea. Following the
adoption of the Argentina’s interest in the oceans and
the law of the sea has not changed since the adoption of
the Convention — it remains a priority.
Argentina shares the intention of the initiative
presented by Fiji and Sweden to provide for the
follow-up of the implementation of Goal 14 of the
2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development (resolution
70/1). However, we do not share the proposed method.
In the process undertaken in the High-level Political
Forum on Sustainable Development, an understanding
was reached that the follow-up to the implementation
of the 16 Goals would be carried out on the basis of
existing structures and would avoid duplication and the
creation of new structures. Argentina does not agree
that a high-level conference of the United Nations is
the way to achieve that, because the implementation of
the Goals related to the conservation and sustainable
use of the oceans and seas and their resources requires
the involvement of bodies in which technical experts
responsible for implementation at the national level
also participate. Argentina will be actively involved in
negotiating the modalities for the conference provided
for in the resolution, in order to ensure that there will
be no overlaps with competent forums on the oceans

and law of the sea, including the United Nations Openended Informal Consultative Process on Oceans and the
Law of the Sea (UNICPOLOS), which was established
by the General Assembly.
In that connection, Argentina joined the consensus
for the convening of the conference to be organized
by Fiji and Sweden, on the understanding that its
purpose is only to provide political momentum for the
follow-up to Goal 14, and not to negotiate outcomes or
recommendations, and also with the understanding that
no further conferences of that kind would result from
it, which would only generate parallel and overlapping
structures to the existing forums.
My delegation believes that there is a natural forum
for Goal 14, which was created after the World Summit
on Sustainable Development precisely to address oceans
issues from the perspective of sustainable development
and which falls under the authority of the General
Assembly, namely, the United Nations Open-ended
Informal Consultative Process on Oceans and the Law
