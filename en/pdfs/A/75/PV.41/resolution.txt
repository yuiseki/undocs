Reports of the Special Political and Decolonization
Committee (Fourth Committee)
The President: Before we proceed, I would like to
make some opening remarks.
At the outset, I would like to thank the Chair of
the Special Political and Decolonization Committee
(Fourth Committee), His Excellency Mr. Collen Vixen
Kelapile, Permanent Representative of Botswana
to the United Nations, and the other members of the
Bureau for their able and skilful steering of the Fourth
Committee during this session. My deepest thanks also
go to Member States for successfully accomplishing the
Committee’s extensive programme of work.
The importance of the agenda items of the Fourth
Committee cannot be overstated. On decolonization,
we continue to make efforts to ensure the selfdetermination of all colonial countries and peoples
is implemented in accordance with the Charter of the
United Nations. On peacekeeping, we need to do our
utmost to protect peacekeepers, who risk their own lives
to protect others and fulfil their mandates. Palestinian
issues, which have been on the agenda of the United
Nations since its creation, still require sustained
engagement by the international community to uphold
the rights of the Palestinian people. And on our outer
space, we need to find ways to reap the benefits of
science and technology to invest in the 2030 Agenda
for Sustainable Development.

Owing to the impacts of the coronavirus disease,
this session has seen limitations placed on in-person
meetings. I extend my congratulations to the Bureau of
the Fourth Committee for its pragmatism in proceeding
with a hybrid working model that includes in-person
meetings. I also thank the Secretariat for its efforts to
facilitate those meetings.
I now request the Rapporteur of the Committee,
Mr. Jassim Sayar Al-Maawda of Qatar, to introduce in
one intervention the reports of the Committee.
Mr. Al-Maawda (Qatar) (spoke in Arabic),
Rapporteur of the Special Political and Decolonization
Committee (Fourth Committee): I have the honour
to introduce to the General Assembly the reports of
the Special Political and Decolonization Committee
(Fourth Committee) submitted under agenda items
50 to 61, 126 and 142. These reports, contained in
documents A/75/409 to A/75/422, include the texts of
draft resolutions and decisions recommended to the
General Assembly for adoption. For the convenience
of delegations, the Secretariat prepared a checklist of
actions taken in the Special Political and Decolonization
Committee (Fourth Committee), which is contained in
document A/C.4/75/INF/3.
During the main part of the seventy-fifth session,
the Special Political and Decolonization Committee
(Fourth Committee) held a total of 10 formal meetings.
It also held two virtual informal meetings owing to the
coronavirus disease (COVID-19) pandemic. During the
course of the session, the Committee adopted 34 draft
resolutions and three draft decisions.

The first report, submitted under agenda item 50,
“Effects of atomic radiation”, is contained in document
A/75/409. In the draft resolution contained in paragraph
8 of the report, the General Assembly notes, among
other things, the decision of the Bureau of the United
Nations Scientific Committee on the Effects of Atomic
Radiation to postpone the sixty-seventh session from
July 2020 to November 2020, as a consequence of the
pandemic, in order to allow for the fullest participation
possible under these difficult circumstances, and
also notes that the reports of the sixty-seventh and
sixty-eighth sessions of the Scientific Committee
will be presented at the seventy-sixth session of the
General Assembly.
The second report, submitted under agenda
item 51, “International cooperation in the peaceful uses
of outer space”, is contained in document A/75/410. The
Committee was not able to adopt its annual omnibus
draft resolution owing to the inability of the Committee
on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space (COPUOS) to meet
this year as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic. Instead,
the Fourth Committee adopted a draft decision that
would ensure the continuity of the work of COPUOS
and its subsidiary bodies until those bodies are able
to resume their work in 2021. This draft decision is
contained in paragraph 10 of the report.
During its consideration of the same item, the
Fourth Committee held one virtual informal meeting
of the Working Group of the Whole, chaired by the
delegation of Romania.
The third report, submitted under agenda item 52,
“United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine
Refugees in the Near East”, is contained in document
A/75/411. The Fourth Committee also considered
the report of the Commissioner-General of UNRWA
(A/75/13), as well as other relevant reports. The Fourth
Committee adopted three draft resolutions related to
various aspects of the work of UNRWA, as contained
in paragraph 12 of the report.
The fourth report, submitted under agenda item 53,
“Report of the Special Committee to Investigate Israeli
Practices Affecting the Human Rights of the Palestinian
People and Other Arabs of the Occupied Territories”,
is contained in document A/75/412. The Fourth
Committee also considered the fifty-second report of
the Special Committee concerning the protection and
promotion of the human rights of the Palestinian people
and other Arab inhabitants of the occupied territories,

as well as other reports by the Secretary-General. The

The fifth report, relating to agenda item 54,
“Comprehensive review of the whole question
of peacekeeping operations in all their aspects”,
is contained in document A/75/413. The Fourth

The sixth report of the Committee, relating to agenda
item 55, “Comprehensive review of special political
missions”, is contained in document A/75/414. By the
terms of the draft resolution contained in paragraph 8,
the Assembly requests the Secretary-General to submit
to the General Assembly at its seventy-sixth session
a report on the overall policy matters pertaining to
special political missions, including efforts towards
improving expertise and effectiveness, transparency,
accountability, geographical representation, gender
perspective and the equal participation of women, as
well as youth participation.
The seventh report, submitted under agenda item
56, “Questions relating to information”, is contained in
document A/75/415. The Fourth Committee considered
the report submitted by the Committee on Information
(A/75/21) and the related report of the Secretary-General
and was briefed by the Under-Secretary-General for
Global Communications about the efforts being made
by her Department to promote the United Nations
message around the world. The Fourth Committee
adopted draft resolution A, “Information in the service
of humanity”, and draft resolution B, “United Nations
global communications policies and activities”. Both
draft resolutions are contained in paragraph 8 of
the report.
