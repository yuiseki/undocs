Mr. Aho-Glele (Benin) (spoke in French): On
behalf of the least developed countries, we have
requested the floor in explanation of vote in order to
express our surprise, our deep concern and our
unanimous disappointment at the request made by one
delegation to request a separate vote on operative
paragraph 12 of this draft resolution on commodities.
We would like to emphasize that that provision is
but an expression, mutatis mutandis, of the
commitment entered into by the international
community, in particular the development partners of
the least developed countries, at the Third United
Nations Conference on the Least Developed Countries,
held in Brussels in 2001. Let me recall that
commitment:
“We aim at improving preferential market
access for [least developed countries] by working
towards the objective of duty-free and quota-free
market access for all [least developed countries’]
products in the markets of developed countries”.
(A/CONF.191/12, para. 6)
While we wish to renew our sincere gratitude to
all those partners who have and will continue working
towards improving preferential access by least
developed countries to their markets, we have to point
out that the least developed countries, individually or
collectively, constitute no threat to the competitiveness
of products of those countries due to their structural
weakness in responding to market demands.
Furthermore, least developed countries hardly begin to
develop their productive capacities when they are
deemed able to graduate from the group of least
developed countries, thus losing the benefits derived
from preferential market access.
In such circumstances, any attempt to call into
question a commitment that has been internationally
and unanimously undertaken in favour of the least
developed countries or to seek unilaterally to suppress
it can only fuel and sustain, on the one hand, confusion
and inconsistency in such international initiatives, and,
on the other hand, vague attempts to subjugate the least
developed countries, thus resulting in exploitation of
the dire poverty of the most vulnerable segment of the
international community.
In addition, improved access for least developed
countries to the markets of developed countries is part
of a series of specific measures and commitments taken
under the Brussels Programme of Action, which has

been inadequately implemented since its adoption in
2001 — a fact that is reflected in the relevant
resolutions of the Economic and Social Council and the
General Assembly.
We believe that the request for a separate vote on
operative paragraph 12 of the draft resolution is an
error of judgement. That is why, while there is still
time, we wish to make an appeal through you, Mr.
President, to the delegation that made that request to

tirelessly to improve the fate of humanity, especially
the most vulnerable sectors, to reaffirm their solidarity
and commitment to the least developed countries,
based on the international community’s commitment to
combat extreme poverty and human misery.

have approached us to express their concern over our
vote, believing that it represented a change of policy on
the part of Canada with respect to market access for
products originating from least developed countries.
Nothing could be further from the truth.
Canada’s market access initiative for least
developed countries, which took effect on 1 January
2003, extends duty-free and quota-free access to
Canada’s imports from least developed countries with
the exception of certain over-quota supply-managed
agricultural products, a category which is limited to
dairy and poultry products and eggs. It is important to
note that this initiative is one of the most far-reaching
preference schemes for least developed countries of
any developed country, partly because of its realistic
and liberal rules of origin, which allow for
accumulation from all developing countries.
As a final point, that initiative has had a notable
effect on our trade with least developed countries. In
2003, our total imports from least developed countries
rose by 77 per cent over their 2002 levels. That
includes an increase of 4,307 per cent in imports under
the least-developed-countries preferences. That clearly
shows the degree to which least developed countries
have made use of this Canadian initiative.
We trust that this explanation of vote has clarified
Canada’s position on this issue.
