Mrs. Balázs (Hungary), Rapporteur of the First
Committee: I have the honour to introduce to the
General Assembly the reports of the Disarmament and
International Security Committee, the First Committee,
on agenda items 89 to 105, 121 and 136, as contained in
documents A/74/360 to A/74/377. For the convenience
of delegations, a list of the proposals contained in the
reports of the First Committee has been prepared by
the Secretariat and issued as document A/C.1/74/
INF/3/Rev.1. The revised version includes all proposed
amendments and changes to the votes requested for
consideration at today’s plenary meeting.
During the main part of the seventy-fourth session,
the Disarmament and International Security Committee
held a total of 27 formal meetings to consider the 20
agenda items that were allocated to it. The severe
liquidity crisis at the United Nations forced us to cancel
all the additional meetings that had been envisaged and
to manage time very strictly. Despite the significant

amount of time spent on discussions not necessarily
related to the substantive work of the First Committee,
132 delegations made statements during the general
debate segment, compared to 135 delegations in 2018.
Also, an impressive 347 interventions were made
during the thematic discussion segment, compared to
the 354 interventions made in 2018, despite two fewer
meetings being dedicated to the segment compared to
previous years.
During the action phase, the Committee adopted
59 substantive draft resolutions and decisions — one
was withdrawn and another was not adopted after a
vote — 40 of which were adopted as a whole by recorded
votes, with 58 separate votes requested. Twenty-two
draft proposals were adopted as a whole without a vote,
of which 19 were not voted on at all, either on separate
paragraphs or as a whole.
Of the 59 draft resolutions and decisions adopted
by the Committee, 22 were under the cluster “Nuclear
weapons”. Under the cluster “Other weapons of mass
destruction”, three draft resolutions are recommended.
In addition, four drafts are recommended under the
cluster “Outer space (disarmament aspects)”, eight
under “Conventional weapons”, 10 under “Other
disarmament measures and international security”, five
under “Regional disarmament and security” and, finally,
eight under the cluster “Disarmament machinery”.
The Committee adopted 10 new draft resolutions this
year, thereby strengthening and developing the system
of arms control, disarmament and non-proliferation
treaties and agreements, including a resolution on

In conclusion, and following the dry numerical data,
please allow me to share my own thoughts regarding
the seventy-fourth session of the First Committee.
As I stated earlier, the Committee voted on 40 of the
59 drafts. That is an awful lot. Every year there is
an increasing number of votes on First Committee
draft resolutions and decisions, which tends to mirror
the conflicts in the world. It seems that what our
predecessors built up through long, hard work, namely,
an operating mechanism that protected generations
from world wars, is now being weakened. We are close
to the brink and are risking our future and that of our
children and their children.
The First Committee is one of the main forums
where States are called upon to discuss and resolve
their disagreements in the field of non-proliferation
and disarmament. Yet, at the moment, we are having
difficulties with it. I have seen delegations successfully
work together during the session, so I will dare to say
that we can still stop at the brink and contemplate how
we wish to proceed.
