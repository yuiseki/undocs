Annex
Monthly report to the United Nations on the operations of the
Kosovo Force
1.

This report covers the period from 1 to 31 July 2004.

2.
As at 31 July 2004, the total number of troops in theatre was 17,728, including
3,068 troops from non-Nato countries.
3.

There was no change of status of partner/non-NATO country contributions.

Security
4.
The general situation in Kosovo is relatively calm but not stable, with the
potential for inter-ethnic violence. The public protest over decentralization in the
former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia1 has not spread to Kosovo; KFOR is
monitoring this situation closely.
5.
The number of violent intra- and inter-ethnic events is low. During the month
four incidents were reported, which were directed towards Kosovo Serbs or Ashkalis
by Kosovo Albanians.
6.

The threat against KFOR remained relatively low throughout the month.

7.
A total of 59 incidents were reported related to drugs, weapons and smuggling
activity in July 2004. This was a 37 per cent increase compared to the previous
month. Events related to drugs, smuggling and counterfeit currency were increasing,
while illegal weapons finds were decreasing.
8.
KFOR continued operations to secure the theatre to avoid ethnic violence and
protect minority enclaves and patrimonial sites. KFOR remained vigilant to deter
possible threats directed against international organizations and military bases. The
NATO force continued to improve its crowd and riot control capabilities.
Compliance by the Army of Serbia and Montenegro and the Ministry of Internal
Affairs Special Police
9.
No breaches of the Military Technical Agreement were reported during July
2004.
Kosovo Protection Corps
10. The current membership of the Kosovo Protection Corps (KPC) is 2,986 active
members, including 142 minorities, of which 32 are Serbs. The reserve personnel
establishment list includes 1,838 members, of which 17 are minorities. The results
show a low rate of minorities in KPC. A concern is that there are not enough free
positions in the active personnel establishment list for minorities to fill in order to
reach the 10 per cent minority goal for KPC.
11. During July 2004, roll calls were conducted in 29 KPC units. The number of
individuals on sick leave, or other, is at an acceptable level of approximately 20 per
cent.
