Annex
Letter dated 17 December 1999 from the Director General
of the International Atomic Energy Agency addressed to
the Secretary-General

As you will recall, in its presidential statement of 4 November 1994
(S/PRST/1994/64), regarding the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea (DPRK),
the Security Council, inter alia, requested the International Atomic Energy
Agency (IAEA) "to continue to report to it on the implementation of the
Safeguards Agreement". In this regard, I enclose a copy of my written report
about the DPRK (dated 25 August 1999) submitted to the General Conference of
IAEA at its forty-third regular session (see enclosure I) and of resolution
GC(43)/Res/3, adopted by the Conference on 1 October 1999 (see enclosure II).
Since my letter to you of 9 October 1998 (see S/1998/940), the Agency has
continued to monitor the freeze of the DPRK’s graphite moderated reactors and
related facilities foreseen in the Agreed Framework of October 1994 between the
DPRK and the United States of America. The DPRK continues to accept Agency
activities solely within the context of the Agreed Framework and not under its
Safeguards Agreement with the Agency, which, as you know, is binding and remains
in force.
Two further rounds of technical discussions between IAEA and the DPRK were
held between my letter to you of October last year and this year’s General
Conference, namely, from 5 to 8 October 1998 in Vienna and from 10 to
12 March 1999 in Pyongyang. Unfortunately, however, no progress was made on
important issues relevant to the DPRK’s compliance with its Safeguards
Agreement, including those identified in my letter of 9 October 1998, such as
the preservation of information required in connection with Agency verification
of the accuracy and completeness of the DPRK’s initial declaration. I have made
clear to the Agency Board of Governors that, unless such information is
preserved in a verifiable manner, it will be difficult, if not impossible, for
the Agency to verify the DPRK’s compliance with its Safeguards Agreement.
At meetings of the Board of Governors throughout the year, I have reported
the continuing lack of progress over key issues. At the meeting on 7 June 1999,
I also noted that the Agency’s Safeguards Implementation Report for 1998
recorded the continuing inability of the Agency to verify the correctness and
completeness of the DPRK’s initial declaration of nuclear material and,
therefore, our inability to conclude that there has been no diversion of nuclear
material.
In the light of the information available at that time, and of my report to
this year’s General Conference, the Conference adopted resolution GC(43)/Res/3.
This, in keeping with previous resolutions, expressed the deep concern of the
Conference about the continuing non-compliance of the DPRK with its Safeguards
Agreement, urged the DPRK to cooperate fully with the Agency in the
implementation of that agreement, and also to take all steps the Agency may deem
necessary to preserve all information relevant to verifying the accuracy and
completeness of the DPRK’s initial report.

We have just completed the thirteenth round of technical discussions with
the DPRK. They took place in Vienna from 13 to 16 December. During the
meeting, little tangible progress was made on some of the key issues related to
the preservation of information and to day-to-day activities related to the
monitoring of the freeze.
I should be grateful if you would bring the text of the present letter and
its enclosures to the attention of the Security Council, to which I will
continue to report developments.

(Signed)

Mohamed ELBARADEI
