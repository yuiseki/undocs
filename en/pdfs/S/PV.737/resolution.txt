1888 Convention. 1 It is therefore necessary for us, with

a balance between the rights and obligations of the
parties signatories to these treaties. But th Convention

international community without distinction of flag
and-what is most pertinent-regardless of any states

is a typical example of treaty-Iaw, of a -::reaty which
creates general obligations, of a treaty conc1uded, we
might say, sub specie humanitatis et non sub specie
partium: a treaty made under the aegis of mankind,
and required to operate for the benefit of mankind. But
an analysis of the treaty shows it to be of this type,
