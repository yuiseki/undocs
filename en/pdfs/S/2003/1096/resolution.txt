Letter dated 11 November 2003 from the Secretary-General
addressed to the President of the Security Council
I have the honour to refer to my letter of 5 August 2002 (S/2002/916), in
which I proposed, and the Security Council subsequently approved (see
S/2002/917), the extension of the mandate of the United Nations Peace-building
Support Office in Guinea-Bissau (UNOGBIS) from 31 December 2002 until 31
December 2003.
During 2003, as members of the Council are aware, UNOGBIS centred its
activities on helping manage the multiple crises that had arisen as a result of
institutional differences among various State organs over their constitutional
responsibilities, especially following the dissolution of the National Assembly by
former President Kumba Yalá in November 2002. The Guinea-Bissau military cited
these crises as reason for their intervention on 14 September 2003. Transitional
arrangements were subsequently established, including the appointment of a civilian
President.
In his letter to me of 5 November 2003, President Henrique Perreira Rosa
requested the extension of the mandate of UNOGBIS until 31 December 2004 to
facilitate dialogue among all actors and to promote national reconciliation during the
transition.
I propose, therefore, that the mandate of UNOGBIS be extended for one year,
until 31 December 2004. Following consultations with the transitional authorities,
and in the light of the current situation on the ground, the present mandate has been
revised as follows:
(a) To support all efforts to promote political dialogue, national
reconciliation, the rule of law and respect for human rights, and to strengthen
democratic institutions;
(b) To encourage the Government and other national stakeholders to restore
and consolidate constitutional normality and to create a conducive environment for
peace, stability and the holding of free and transparent elections;
(c) To assist with these elections, in close cooperation with the United
Nations country team and other international partners;
(d) To support national efforts, including those of civil society and in
particular women’s organizations, aimed at strengthening the country’s capacities
