Letter dated 17 March 2011 from the Chairman of the
Security Council Working Group on Children and Armed
Conflict addressed to the Secretary-General
On 8 September 2010, the Security Council Working Group on Children and
Armed Conflict examined the fourth report of the Secretary-General on children and
armed conflict in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (S/2010/369). At its 29th
meeting, on 25 February 2011, the Working Group adopted its conclusions on
children and armed conflict in the Democratic Republic of the Congo
(S/AC.51/2011/1).
In follow-up to the recommendations of the Working Group, approved by the
Security Council, and subject to and consistent with applicable international law and
relevant Security Council resolutions, including Security Council resolutions 1612
(2005) and 1882 (2009), I am entrusted, in my capacity as the Chairman of the
Working Group, to recognize the critical role that the United Nations Organization
Stabilization Mission in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (MONUSCO) and
the United Nations have played in the protection of children in the Democratic
Republic of the Congo.
I am also entrusted to recall that, in line with the conditionality policy
described in Security Council resolution 1906 (2009), logistical and technical
support of MONUSCO to the Forces armées de la République démocratique du
Congo (FARDC) in all FARDC-led military operations against foreign and
Congolese armed groups may be provided only if the operations are conducted in
compliance with international humanitarian, human rights and refugee law.
The Working Group stresses the need for MONUSCO, through its Resident
Coordinator/Humanitarian Coordinator function, to redouble its efforts for longterm reintegration support.
Lastly, the Working Group requests you to ensure that action plans with
various parties are implemented without further delay, and that the Council should
be apprised immediately in the event that further difficulties arise.
(Signed) Peter Wittig
Chairman of the Security Council Working Group
on Children and Armed Conflict
