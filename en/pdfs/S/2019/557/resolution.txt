Letter dated 10 July 2019 from the President of the
Security Council addressed to the Secretary-General
Further to the letter dated 24 May 2019 from Ambassador Dian Triansyah Djani,
I have the honour to confirm that the members of the Security Council have agreed
to send a mission to Colombia from 11 to 14 July 2019. The members have agreed on
the terms of reference of the mission (see annex).
The mission will be co-led by Ambassador Gustavo Meza-Cuadra (Peru) and
Ambassador Jonathan Guy Allen (United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern
Ireland).
Following consultations with the members, it has been agreed that the
composition of the mission will be as follows:

Annex
Terms of reference of the Security Council mission to Colombia,
11 to 14 July 2019
1.
The Security Council will carry out its mission to Colombia within the context
of Council resolutions 2366 (2017), 2377 (2017) and 2435 (2018). The mission will
be co-led by Peru and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland.
2.

The objectives of the mission are as follows:

(a) To demonstrate the Security Council’s full commitment to the peace
process in Colombia and its support for the implementation of the Final Agreement
for Ending the Conflict and Building a Stable and Lasting P eace;
(b) To observe and support the efforts of the United Nations Verification
Mission in Colombia to implement its mandate, in line with Council resolutions 2366
(2017), 2377 (2017) and 2435 (2018);
(c) To acknowledge the importance of the end of the conflict with the former
Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia-People’s Army, to recognize the challenges
of building peace after more than 50 years of conflict, to understand the aspirations
and concerns of the parties and other actors and to encourage positive cooperation
and continued momentum in implementing the Agreement for the benefit of all
Colombians.
3.
The mission will seek to achieve the objectives through meetings with
representatives of:
(a)

The Government of Colombia;

(b)

The People’s Alternative Revolutionary Force political party;

(c)

The United Nations Verification Mission in Colombia;

(d)

The Peace Commissions of Congress;

(e) The transitional justice institutions under the Comprehensive System of
Truth, Justice, Reparation and Non-Repetition;
(f)

The United Nations country team in Colombia;

(g) Members of civil society, community leaders and non-governmental
organizations.
4.
The mission will seek to achieve its objectives through a visit to a territorial
area for training and reintegration where former combatants of the Revolutionary
Armed Forces of Colombia-People’s Army are undergoing their reintegration, which
will include meetings with residents of the territorial area for training and
reintegration, authorities and relevant civil society and community leaders.
5.
The mission will also seek to implement lessons learned from previous Security
Council missions.
