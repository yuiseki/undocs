Enclosure
Monthly report to the United Nations on the operations of the
Kosovo Force
1.

This report covers the period from 1 to 30 April 2007.

2.
As at 30 April 2007, the total number of troops in theatre was 15,796, which
included 2,520 troops from non-NATO countries and the temporarily deployed
operational reserve battalion.
3.

There was no change to the status of partner/non-NATO country contributions.

Political situation
4.
Discussions in the Security Council on President Ahtisaari’s plan for a Kosovo
future status were held on 3 April 2007. Reactions to the plan from the Kosovo
Albanian side were positive, whereas the Kosovo Serbs increasingly pressured their
political actors to position themselves against the plan. In this regard, the Municipal
Assembly of Leposavic adopted a text declaring the status proposal unacceptable,
calling for renewed negotiations, and condemning the support of UNMIK and the
Kosovo Force (KFOR).
5.
The 15 members of the Security Council carried out a fact-finding mission to
Belgrade and Kosovo from 26 to 28 April 2007. No incidents were reported. The
Security Council delegation met with key representatives of all ethnicities. During
the visit, an estimated 3,000 to 4,000 Kosovo-Serbs gathered at the Kosovo
administrative boundary line to protest against the Ahtisaari plan and to demand to
return to their homes. It was later assessed that many of those that had gathered at
the administrative boundary line already live in Kosovo.
Security situation
6.

No incidents of ethnic violence were reported in April 2007.

7.

No incidents of violence or threats against KFOR were recorded in April 2007.

8.
A total of 300 incidents related to unexploded ordnance (126), illegal weapons
possession (71), weapons and ammunition findings (31), drugs (13), counterfeit
currency (7), smuggling (9), human trafficking (1) and miscellaneous criminal
incidents (42) were reported during April, compared to 304 in the previous month.
KFOR operations
9.
The operational tempo of KFOR framework operations remained calm in
April. KFOR forces are engaged in framework operations as well as intelligencedriven operations to ensure a safe and secure environment and to remain prepared to
react to potential disturbances Kosovo wide. The German Operational Reserve
Force battalion has completed its operational rehearsal level 3 and redeployed back
to Germany. The Italian Operational Reserve Force battalion will conduct the next
operational rehearsal starting in June 2007.

Kosovo Protection Corps
11. There was no recruitment campaign for the Kosovo Protection Corps (KPC) in
April.
12. The current active personnel establishment is 2,936. There were 201 (6.84 per
cent) active ethnic minority members (174 males, 27 females): 36 Ashkalis, 4 Roma,
12 Egyptians, 15 Croats, 53 Bosniaks, 38 Turks, 1 Goran and 42 Serbs. The total
numbers of ethnic minorities decreased by three members compared to the previous
month. The reserve personnel establishment list includes 2,000 positions, 1,892
filled, of which the ethnic minority number is 48 (2.45 per cent), the same as last
month.
13. KFOR continues to support UNMIK in training KPC on civilian and nonpolitical aspects. The officer career course continues with 17 students, as does the
officer basic course with 27 students. The basic training course, which started with
33 students, now counts only 12 students. Twenty-one of them left for various
reasons, mostly health issues. English language courses continue with a total of 362
students. KPC also continues with computer course second generation and all other
training as planned. Eighteen students of the third generation staff and command
course graduated successfully on 27 April.
14. During the month of April there were 27 ceremonies in which 582 KPC
members and 530 civilians were involved. No incidents were reported.
Conclusion
15. The overall situation in Kosovo is calm but the political uncertainty related to
the future status could raise some tensions in the near term. KFOR is prepared for
all contingencies and stands ready to maintain safety and security in Kosovo. The
threat level against UNMIK and other international community facilities remains
medium, and the threat level against KFOR remains low.
