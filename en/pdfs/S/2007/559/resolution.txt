Enclosure
Monthly report to the United Nations on the operations of
the Kosovo Force
1.

This report covers the period from 1 to 31 July 2007.

2.
As at 30 July 2007, the total number of troops in theatre was 15,350. This
includes 2,482 troops from non-NATO countries.
3.
In terms of the status of partner/non-NATO country contributions, it should be
noted that Argentina has withdrawn the last of its staff officers in the headquarters
of the Kosovo Force (KFOR).
Political situation
4.
After attempts to achieve a new Security Council resolution on Kosovo were
temporarily suspended on 20 July 2007, the Contact Group agreed, on 25 July, to
open a new period of negotiations between Belgrade and Pristina. The Commander
of KFOR, Lieutenant General Roland Kather, participated on 9 July in an informal
discussion on the current security situation in Kosovo with the Security Council in
New York.
5.
In spite of persistent tension in Kosovo as a result of continued uncertainty
over the outcome of the future status process, the summer has been quiet securitywise. Water shortages remain a priority for municipal leaders, particularly given the
recent forest fires. The insufficient amount of electrical power remains of concern,
as the need to import electricity incurred a financial deficit.
Security situation
6.
No incidents of violence or threats against KFOR were recorded in June 2007
and the threat level against KFOR is assessed as low.
7.
A total of 337 incidents related to drugs (26), counterfeit currency (33), human
trafficking (1), smuggling (15), explosions (2), miscellaneous criminal incidents
(95), illegal weapons possession (61), weapons and ammunitions found (15) and
unexploded ordnance (89) were reported during this period, compared to 306
incidents during the previous month.
KFOR operations
8.
The operational tempo remained steady. KFOR remains vigilant in maintaining
a safe and secure environment and freedom of movement through the conduct of
framework operations as well as intelligence-driven operations. During the month of
July, KFOR conducted 61 search operations, 14,792 vehicle checks and 210 joint
activities. The Italian KFOR Battalion redeployed following an operational
rehearsal completed on 21 July. Continued vigilance is maintained to detect any
signs of disturbance.
Compliance
9.
On 26 July, the Disciplinary Board decided on three major acts of
non-compliance with the statement of principles of the KFOR Commander and
UNMIK regulation 1999/8. The first was a case of misuse of Kosovo Protection

KPC member. The other acts of non-compliance were two cases of unauthorized

Kosovo Protection Corps
10.

There was no recruitment campaign during July 2007.

11. The current active personnel establishment is 2,914. There are 196 (6.83 per
cent) active ethnic minority members (172 male, 24 female). The percentage of
minority members within KPC remains short of the goal set by the Special
Representative of the Secretary-General and there are 113 open slots left in the
active personnel establishment list. The reserve list includes 2,000 positions, of
which 1,892 are already filled (including 48 ethnic minority members, which
equates to 2.45 per cent).
12. During the reporting period, KFOR has been deployed, in support of KPC, to
numerous wildfires across Kosovo. KPC conducted 70 firefighting operations
involving more than 600 KPC members and approximately 50 vehicles.
13. Current training activities continue as planned and future training
opportunities were discussed at meetings involving KPC, the Office of the KPC
Coordinator and KFOR. KPC is still waiting for a donor country to increase the
number of English language classes for the rest of 2007.
14. During the month of July there were six ceremonies involving 179 KPC
members and 300 civilians. No incidents were reported.
Conclusion
15. The situation remains calm and quiet but the political uncertainty related to the
future status talks may raise tension in the short term. Energy and water shortages
remain a priority for municipal leaders and have worsened over the past month
because of widespread forest fires. KFOR offered manpower and aircraft support to
assist in dealing with the fires.
