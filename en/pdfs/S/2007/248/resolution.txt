Enclosure
Monthly report to the United Nations on the operations
of the Kosovo Force
1.

This report covers the period from 1 to 28 February 2007.

2.
As at 25 February 2007, the total number of troops in theatre was 15,085,
which included 2,532 troops from non-NATO countries.
3.
There was no change to the status of NATO/NATO partner/non-NATO country
contributions.
Political situation
4.
The United Nations Special Envoy, Martti Ahtisaari, presented his draft
comprehensive proposal for a Kosovo status settlement to Belgrade and Pristina on
2 February 2007. Reactions in Kosovo were mixed, Kosovo Albanian political
leaders reacted positively to the Special Envoy’s proposal. Kosovo Serbs rejected
the proposal as did radical Kosovo Albanians. A violent demonstration, organized by
the Movement for Self-Determination (MSD) in Pristina on 10 February 2007,
targeted Mr. Ahtisaari’s proposal. The demonstration resulted in two deaths, and the
MSD leader, Albin Kurti, was arrested. As a consequence of the events, the Minister
of Internal Affairs of Kosovo, Fatmir Rexhepi, resigned on 12 February 2007.
Shortly thereafter, on 14 February 2007, UNMIK Police Commissioner Steven
Curtis also resigned upon the request of the Special Representative of the SecretaryGeneral.
5.
Former Kosovo Prime Minister Ramush Haradinaj arrived in The Hague on
26 February 2007, to appear before the International Tribunal for the Former
Yugoslavia on war crimes charges. Before his departure, he met with all Kosovo
Albanian political leaders and with Special Representative of the Secretary-General
Ruecker. In his statement at the Alliance for the Future of Kosovo (AAK)
presidency meeting, Haradinaj called on the population to be responsible and to
refrain from violence. The bombing of a United Nations vehicle in Pristina on
19 February 2007, alleged to have been carried out by the “renewed Kosovo
Liberation Army (KLA)”, was condemned by all political forces in Kosovo, as well
as the Kosovo Protection Corps Headquarters.
Security situation
6.

During the reporting period, no inter-ethnic incidents were reported.

7.
During the reporting period, no incidents against the Kosovo Force (KFOR)
were recorded.
8.
A total of 262 incidents related to unexploded ordnance (76), illegal weapons
possession (105), weapons and ammunition findings (16), drugs (20), counterfeit
currency (20), smuggling (21) and human trafficking (4) were reported during the
past month, compared to 308 in the previous month.
9.
KFOR continued to conduct framework operations. The operational tempo
remained high in anticipation of possible demonstrations, popular reactions to the
final status and further reactions from the 10 February 2007 demonstration in
Pristina. KFOR continued to gather intelligence and improve situational awareness

regarding reactions to the future status proposal. Multinational Task Forces
maintained a reinforced presence in specific areas to deter any potential violence
and to help maintain a safe and secure environment. KFOR activity in the sector of

Compliance
10. Two major cases of non-compliance with the statement of principles of the
KFOR Commander and UNMIK Regulation 1999/8 were decided at the Disciplinary
Board meeting of 19 February. Brigadier General Nuredin Lushtaku was suspended
for four months, with pay, until the next meeting of the Disciplinary Board, and was
fined 20 per cent of his salary. Captain Agim Buja was sanctioned and received a
written warning and a delay in promotion.
Kosovo Protection Corps
11. The current active personnel establishment is 3,030. There are 210 (6.93 per
cent) active ethnic minority members (180 males, 30 females), 36 Ashkalis,
4 Romas, 12 Egyptians, 19 Croats, 53 Bosniaks, 38 Turks, 1 Goran and 47 Serbs.
The total number of ethnic minorities decreased by 1 member (Kosovo Serb),
compare to last month.
12. During the month of February there were nine ceremonies, which involved
more than 110 KPC members and more than 900 civilians. No incidents were
reported.
Conclusion
13. The overall situation in Kosovo remains calm but the political and diplomatic
uncertainty related to the future status process may cause tension in the near term.
KFOR remains fully ready to support UNMIK and the local authorities in their
response to potential unrest as the situation evolves. The threat level against
UNMIK and other international community facilities remains medium, and the
threat level against KFOR remains low.
