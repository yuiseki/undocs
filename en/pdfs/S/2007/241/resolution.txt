Letter dated 27 March 2007 from the Secretary-General to the
President of the Security Council
I have the honour to refer to my letters of 10 July 2006 (S/2006/510) and
8 December 2006 (S/2007/46) with respect to the processing of letters of credit
pertaining to the oil-for-food programme.
In response to the above-mentioned letters, you had requested that the
Controller of the United Nations consult with the relevant Iraqi authorities with a
view to solving the pending issues.
In this respect, the relevant authorities of the Government of Iraq met with the
Controller in Amman on 6 March 2007. Please find attached a note outlining the
main outcomes of that meeting and recommended course of action.
(Signed) Ban Ki-Moon

Note by the Secretary-General on arrangements for terminating
the operations relating to the letters of credit raised against
the United Nations Iraq Account under Security Council
resolution 1483 (2003)
1.
As shown in annex I, there are 55 letters of credit with a total value of
approximately $226.9 million which the Government of Iraq and the United Nations
Secretariat have agreed to cancel. In addition, there are 59 letters of credit with a
total value of $75.1 million which the concerned Ministries have asked the United
Nations Secretariat to cancel, and against which subsequent review has indicated
that there are claims of delivery. Therefore, these letters of credit will not be
cancelled until it is verified that there are no pending claims.
2.
The concerned Ministries have also asked for the extension of 85 letters of
credit until the end of 2007. The Government of Iraq has been asked to transmit
expeditiously all of the requests for amendments to the United Nations through the
Central Bank of Iraq. In this regard, it was emphasized that it would not be possible
for the United Nations Secretariat to support any extension that result in delivery
beyond 31 December 2007.
3.
Furthermore, there are currently 184 letters of credit, with an approximate
value of $182 million, which expired in 2004, 2005 and 2006 (see annex VII) and
for which there are claims of delivery from the suppliers. As was indicated in the
Controller’s letter of 2 February 2007 to the Permanent Representative of Iraq to the
United Nations, most of the suppliers presented their documents to the Bank holding
the United Nations Iraq Account, and the Bank was not able to make payments
promptly to suppliers according to the terms and conditions of the letters of credit
for lack of authentication documents for the arrival of goods in Iraq. As agreed at
the meeting in Amman, the Government of Iraq was requested to transmit the
necessary authentication documents expeditiously.
4.
The attention of the Security Council is also drawn to the issue of allegations
of improper withholding of authentication documents as referred to in the letter of
the Secretary-General dated 8 December 2006 (S/2007/46) and the related letter of
the President of the Security Council dated 29 January 2007 (S/2007/47). To date,
the Secretariat has not received any indication as to developments regarding the
allegations. This situation presents a serious risk to the possibility of a timely
termination of the programme.
Action sought of the Security Council
5.
The Council may wish to decide whether to support the proposal for
extensions of letters of credit as indicated in paragraph 2 above. In addition, the
Council may wish to state its firm position regarding the urgent need for an early
solution to the chronic problem of slow provision or non-provision of authentication
documents. Finally, the Council may wish to urge the Government of Iraq to resolve
expeditiously and fully the outstanding allegations regarding improper withholding
of authentication documents so that all due vendor obligations may be met.

Meeting of the United Nations Secretariat and the Iraqi Ministries
regarding the oil-for-food programme
Amman, 6 March 2007
1.
A meeting was held on Tuesday, 6 March 2007 in Amman between the
representatives of the Government of Iraq and the representatives of the United
Nations Secretariat to discuss arrangements for the closing of the oil-for-food
programme under the provisions of Security Council resolution 1483 (2003).
2.
The meeting was briefed on the status of processing of the outstanding letters
of credit as at 31 December 2006. It was noted that 310 letters of credit remained,
with a value of over $915 million. These letters of credit represent the residue of
20,435 letters of credit that were issued by BNP Paribas for the oil-for-food
programme. Of those residual letters of credit, 184 related to items for which there
were claims of delivery but for which authentication documents have not been
submitted — these had a total value of over $182 million. The Secretariat
representative observed that there continued to be complaints from vendors of slow
processing of authentications and that this was also a concern of the Security
Council.
3.
It was also observed that there were many expired letters of credit which in
principle could be cancelled. These were estimated by the Secretariat to number at
least 93, with a total value of over $492 million.
4.
The representative of the Government of Iraq indicated that the total number
of letters of credit which had expired and could be cancelled might exceed 93. In
any case, the record of the Ministries would be matched with the records of the
Secretariat so that arrangements could be made to cancel the related letters of credit
and return the collateral funds currently held by BNP Paribas to the Development
Fund for Iraq.
5.
The representatives of the Government of Iraq indicated that some of the
expired letters of credit would need to be extended in the light of the situation on the
ground in Iraq. Accordingly, requests for extensions would be submitted for later
dates in 2007 and, in some cases, beyond 2007. The representative of the Secretariat
indicated that two previous sets of extensions had been submitted to the Security
Council and had been approved by it in 2005 and 2006. In view of this, and the need
to close the programme under the Security Council resolution, it would not be
possible for the Secretariat to support extensions beyond 2007. The Secretariat
believed that, should contracts not be completed by the end of 2007, it would be
necessary for the residual funds to be returned to the Development Fund for Iraq and
that the Government of Iraq would need to administer the balance of the outstanding
contracts.
6.
The representative of Iraq raised the issue of refund of penalties imposed on
some vendors because of failure to execute the contracts according to the terms and
conditions of the same. The Secretariat representative noted that it was the
established policy not to refund penalties to vendors. It was however noted that, in
some of these cases, non-performance was due to force majeure and the situation
would be further reviewed in New York with the help of the Office of Legal Affairs.

Conclusions
7.

The meeting decided that:

(a) An agreed list of expired letters of credit should be established by the
Secretariat in consultation with the Government of Iraq, and the related contracts
and letters of credit would be cancelled and the funds held in respect of those letters
of credit would be returned to the Development Fund for Iraq in March 2007;
(b) The processing of authentication documents by the Government of Iraq
should be accelerated to ensure that vendors are paid promptly. Particular attention
could be paid to the 184 letters of credit with claims outstanding which were listed
in table 1 (a) of the Controller’s letter dated 2 February 2007. It was acknowledged
by both parties that renewed effort will be needed to shorten the notification period;
(c) The Government of Iraq would submit a list of contracts for which it
wished that an extension of expiration date be sought from the Security Council.
The Secretariat undertook to provide the Council with this information but would
not make a recommendation in respect of such a proposal other than the fact that, if
contracts were not to be fulfilled by 31 December 2007, arrangements should be
made to transfer the related funds to the Development Fund for Iraq, together with
the responsibility for contract administration and settlement, as these would become
the responsibility of the Government of Iraq;
(d) It was agreed that, in a limited number of cases of non-performance due
to force majeure, reference would be made to the Office of Legal Affairs;
(e) The Secretariat undertook to bring these matters to the attention of the
Security Council in March 2007. The Government of Iraq indicated that it would
also be communicating with the Security Council on the above agreement.
