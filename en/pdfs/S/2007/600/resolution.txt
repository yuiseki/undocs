Enclosure
Monthly report to the United Nations on the operations of the
Kosovo Force
1.

This report covers the period from 1 to 31 August 2007.

2.
As at 31 August 2007, the total number of troops in theatre was 15,078. This
includes 2,456 troops from non-NATO countries.
Political situation
3.
The extended period of negotiations on Kosovo’s future status was officially
launched by the United Nations Secretary-General in a public statement on 1 August
2007. The European Union, United States and Russian Federation Troika paid its
first visit to Belgrade and Pristina on 10 and 11 August 2007. A second round of
talks between the parties was held in Vienna on 30 August 2007. There was no
major breakthrough in the negotiations so far as both Belgrade and Pristina retain
their fundamental well-known positions.
4.
Lieutenant General Kather handed over the command of the KFOR forces to
Lieutenant General Bout de Marnhac during a ceremony at KFOR headquarters on
31 August 2007.
Security situation
5.
Despite ongoing diplomatic uncertainty surrounding the future status process,
the month of August was relatively calm. Reported incidents include the killing of
one Kosovo Police Service officer for criminal reasons; a break-in and minor
damage at a Serbian church in Multinational Task Force Centre, and the escape from
Dubrava prison of seven prisoners suspected of possible terrorist activities. There
were rumours of an increased number of Kosovo Serbs buying houses from Kosovo
Albanians north of the River Ibar (Little Bosnia and Three Towers) at high prices.
6.
During August 2007 no incidents against KFOR were recorded. The threat
level against KFOR is low.
7.
A total of 416 incidents related to drugs (23), counterfeit currency (29),
smuggling (13), miscellaneous criminal incidents (99), illegal weapons possession
(118), weapons and ammunitions seizure (68) and unexploded ordnance (66) were
reported during this period. The total number of incidents is significantly higher
than in July (337), the greatest increase being in illegal weapons possession and
weapons seizure.
KFOR operations
8.
The operational tempo remained steady. KFOR continues to help maintain a
safe and secure environment and freedom of movement through the conduct of
framework operations and intelligence-driven operations. KFOR is fully prepared to
react to potential disturbances Kosovo-wide.
Compliance
9.
During the month of August, the KFOR Legal Adviser issued three certificates
for three Kosovo Protection Corps members reported as absent without permission.

The Deputy Commander of the Kosovo Protection Corps issued one suspension
order. The above-mentioned cases will be decided at the next Disciplinary Board
meeting, scheduled for 18 September 2007.
Kosovo Protection Corps
10. There was no recruitment campaign for the Kosovo Protection Corps (KPC) in
August 2007.
11. The current active personnel establishment is 2,914. There are 196 (6.83 per
cent) active ethnic minority members (172 male, 24 female). The total number of
ethnic minorities remains the same as last month. The percentage of minorities
within KPC is still short of the goal set by the Special Representative of the
Secretary-General and there are 113 open slots left in the active personnel
establishment list. The reserve list includes 2,000 positions, of which 1,892 are
filled (including 48 ethnic minority members, which equates 2.45 per cent).
12. KPC is still waiting for a donor country to increase the number of English
language classes for the rest of 2007. The multinational task forces continue lessons
on module-based training in accordance with agreed guidance. The new training
order was released on 4 August 2007 and the new training programme will start on
1 September 2007. The third generation of university studies started on 27 August
2007 with 17 members. Seven KPC-managed projects were conducted throughout
Kosovo.
13. During the month of August, 21 ceremonies involving 484 KPC members were
held. There were no incidents reported.
Conclusion
14. The situation remains relatively calm but the political uncertainty related to the
future status talks may raise tensions in the short term. KFOR remains fully
prepared to deal with any unrest in Kosovo and would respond forcefully to any
attempt to disrupt the political process, regardless of where it comes from. Tensions
in Kosovo could potentially have repercussions in neighbouring areas, particularly
in the northern part of the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia 1 and in the
Presevo Valley.
