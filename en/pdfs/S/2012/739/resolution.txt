Letter dated 4 October 2012 from the Secretary-General
addressed to the President of the Security Council
I have the honour to transmit a letter dated 28 September 2012 from
Kadré Désiré Ouédraogo, President of the Commission of the Economic Community
of West African States (ECOWAS), requesting a mandate from the Security Council
for the deployment of an ECOWAS stabilization force to Mali under Chapter VII of
the Charter of the United Nations (see annex).
I would be grateful if you would bring the present letter and its annex to the
attention of the members of the Security Council.
(Signed) BAN Ki-moon

Annex
Letter dated 28 September 2012 from the President of the
Commission of the Economic Community of West African States
addressed to the Secretary-General
I wish first of all to congratulate and commend you on your initiative to
organize the high-level meeting on the Sahel, which was successfully held on
26 September 2012, and on your decision to appoint a special representative for
the Sahel.
The unanimous view that emerged from the meeting about the need for urgent
and immediate action by the international community to end the rebellion, as well as
the terrorist and criminal activities in the north of Mali, also obliges me to once
more address you on the matter of the Economic Community of West African States
(ECOWAS) request, endorsed by the African Union, for a Security Council
resolution authorizing the deployment of an ECOWAS stabilization force in Mali
under a Chapter VII mandate.
You may want to know that pursuant to Security Council resolution
2056 (2012), ECOWAS intensified its political, diplomatic and military preparatory
efforts, in close cooperation with the African Union, the United Nations and other
partners aimed at satisfying the requirements of the Security Council for a mandate,
as set out in paragraph 18 of the resolution.
In my letter of 1 August 2012, I informed you about progress made by
ECOWAS in stabilizing the transition in Mali, including facilitating the return of the
interim president, Dioncounda Traoré, to Mali on 27 July 2012 and the formation of
a Government of National Unity, as well as the efforts to formulate and implement a
road map that would lead to the reunification of the country and the restoration of
full constitutional order through transparent, free, fair and credible elections by
April 2013.
I also updated you on the outcome of the work of the ECOWAS technical
assessment missions to Mali, with the active participation of military and political
experts from the African Union, the United Nations and the European Union, to
fine-tune the concept, modalities and means of the planned deployment of the
stabilization force.
As you may also be aware, the Government of Mali addressed formal requests
to ECOWAS on 1 and 23 September 2012 for military assistance to end the crisis in
the north of the country.
In the agreement reached with Mali and in line with ECOWAS policy, the
strategy in Mali will be to continue to prioritize and intensify mediation efforts
aimed at a peaceful resolution of the rebellion, while at the same time undertaking a
three-phased deployment process with the following objectives:
Phase I:

Setting up a coordination centre near Bamako to ensure the security
of mission personnel as well as logistical, communications and
other assets

Phase II: Helping the Government to restructure and reorganize the security
and defence forces of Mali

Phase III: Helping the Government to restore the territorial integrity of Mali
by extending State authority to the north, combating terrorist and
criminal networks and responding to the humanitarian
consequences of the crisis
The main purpose of the present letter is, therefore, to seek your cooperation in
securing an urgent meeting of the Security Council to further consider the ECOWAS
request for a resolution authorizing the deployment of a stabilization force in Mali
under a Chapter VII mandate.
As soon as the mandate is granted, ECOWAS will be ready to deploy the first
contingents of the stabilization force to undertake tasks under phases I and II of the
deployment strategy, while fine-tuning the modalities and means for phase III in
cooperation with the African Union, the United Nations and other partners.
I anticipate that the formal request for assistance addressed to ECOWAS by
the Government of Mali clears a major hurdle in the efforts to secure the Security
Council mandate. I am also persuaded to believe, from the deliberations at the
high-level meeting on the Sahel, that the entire international community is
unanimous in considering the crisis in the north of Mali a serious threat to regional
and international peace and security and that, as such, it must not be allowed to
fester any further. Mali today holds the key to tackling the myriad challenges facing
the Sahel, and how the international community cooperates to resolve the double
crisis will provide a template for the implementation of the laudable integrated
strategy for the Sahel.
(Signed) Kadré Désiré Ouédraogo
President of the Commission
