Letter dated 22 July 2009 from the Secretary-General addressed
to the President of the Security Council
I have the honour to refer to article 13 bis, paragraph 2, of the Statute of the
International Tribunal for the Prosecution of Persons Responsible for Serious
Violations of International Humanitarian Law Committed in the Territory of the
Former Yugoslavia since 1991, which reads as follows:
“In the event of a vacancy in the Chambers amongst the permanent judges
elected or appointed in accordance with this article, after consultation with the
Presidents of the Security Council and of the General Assembly, the SecretaryGeneral shall appoint a person meeting the qualifications of article 13 of the
Statute, for the remainder of the term of office concerned.”
In this connection, I should like to recall that Judges Mohamed Shahabuddeen
(Guyana), Iain Bonomy (United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland)
and Christine Van Den Wyngaert (Belgium) have resigned effective 10 May,
31 August and 1 September respectively.
The Government of Belgium has nominated Mr. Guy Delvoie to replace Judge
Van Den Wyngaert. The Government of the United Kingdom has nominated
Mr. Howard Morrison to replace Judge Bonomy. In this regard, please note that
earlier this year, after indicating my intentions to the Security Council, I appointed
Mr. Morrison as judge of the Special Tribunal for Lebanon. However, in the light of
the difficulty of finding judges with the necessary experience to take up the
appointment to the International Tribunal in the required short time frame, and given
that judges of the Special Tribunal are unlikely to start their functions until the first
quarter of 2010, the Government of the United Kingdom has decided to nominate
Mr. Morrison for the position of judge of the International Tribunal. Mr. Morrison
resigned as judge of the Special Tribunal effective 14 July 2009.
As regards the replacement of Judge Shahabuddeen, the Government of
Guyana indicated that it was not in a position to nominate a candidate to replace
him. It has therefore requested that we ask other States of that region for possible
nominees As a result of those consultations, the Government of the Bahamas has
nominated as a permanent judge Sir Burton Hall, currently an ad litem judge at the
International Tribunal not yet assigned to any cases.

Please find attached the curricula vitae of Mr. Guy Delvoie, Mr. Howard
Morrison and Sir Burton Hall (see annex).*
It is my view that the above candidates meet the qualifications prescribed in
article 13 of the Statute of the International Tribunal. I also consider that their
appointment would ensure adequate representation on the Tribunal of the principal
legal systems of the world, as contemplated in article 13 bis 1 (c) of the Statute.
In accordance with article 13 bis, paragraph 2, of the Statute of the
International Tribunal, I look forward to receiving your views on the appointment of
Mr. Delvoie, Mr. Morrison and Sir Burton Hall, as permanent judges of the
International Tribunal.
(Signed) Ban Ki-moon

HHJ Howard Morrison CBE QC
Born Kent UK 1949
Pots-school VSO service Ghana 1968-9 the London University[ law graduate LLB]
Military service [Subaltern, Queen’s Regt and subsequently Para Regt TAVR then RARO]
and then development work in West and Central Africa [VSO officer responsible for
Zambia and Malawi] Post-grad Bar final, Inns of Court School of Law, called to Bar by
Grays Inn in 1977.
Private practice as a barrister from 1977 -1985 doing criminal, civil and family law cases
when appointed as a Resident then Chief Magistrate of Fiji and, concurrently, Senior
Magistrate of Tuvalu. Took Magistracy through first of two military coups intact, the
second coup dismantled the constitution.
1988-89 Served as Attorney General of Anguilla getting anti drugs legislation through a
very hostile Parliament and local legal community 1989- 2004 practice on the UK
Midland Circuit in crime, sitting as a Assistant Recorder [1993] and Recorder [1997] in
crime, family and civil cases. Appointed Queen’s Counsel 2001.
In practice prosecuted cases for the Crown Prosecution Service, Customs and Excise and
the Serious Fraud Office at the highest prosecutorial grade in each case and defended in the
most serious rape and murder cases. Defence practice included Courts Martial in UK and
Germany.
From 1997-2004 defending at the UN International Criminal Tribunals for Yugoslavia and
Rwanda defending in genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes allegations
including two camp commanders [ICTY][Mucic and Nikolic] and a former Rwandan
government Minister [Mugenzi][ICTR] charged with genocide. Created the procedure for
dealing with cases of diminished responsibility at the ICTY in the case of Kovocavic.
Wide knowledge of international criminal and humanitarian and human rights law and a
particular knowledge of the jurisprudence of the ad-hoc Tribunals for the former
Yugoslavia and Rwanda, in particular command and superior responsibility and the three
modes of joint criminal enterprise.
Various publications mostly in the field of International criminal law.
Member of the Race Relations and Equal Opportunities Committees of the Bar Council
1994-2003.
Master of the Bench, Grays Inn 2008
Appointed Circuit Judge December 2004. Authority to try all classes of cases. Extensive
experience of teaching advocacy [Grays Inn] and international criminal, humanitarian and
human rights issues at universities and conferences/seminars in the Middle East [Iraq,
Dubai, Syria, Jordan and Egypt} , Australia [Distinguished Visiting Fellow, Castan Human
Rights Centre, Monash University], Europe [including guest lecturer at the Scoula
Superiore at the University of Cantania, Sicily] 2007 German Bar Conference, 2008
Chaired meeting of the completion strategies for the ICTY/ICTR, European University at
