Annex
Letter dated 12 August 2009 from the Chairman of the Working
Group on Children and Armed Conflict addressed to the
Secretary-General
On 19 December 2008, the Working Group on Children and Armed Conflict,
established by Security Council resolution 1612 (2005), examined your report on
children and armed conflict in Afghanistan (S/2008/695). Following its meeting on
1 July 2009, the Working Group adopted its conclusions on the parties to the armed
conflict in Afghanistan (S/AC.51/2009/1).
In follow-up to the recommendations of the Working Group, approved by the
Security Council, and subject to and consistent with applicable international law and
relevant Security Council resolutions, including resolution 1612 (2005), I am
entrusted, in my capacity as the Chairman of the Working Group, to welcome your
call upon the country-level Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting to present a
plan on ways and means to interface with Government, international military forces
and other relevant parties to extend the monitoring and reporting mechanism
mandated by Council resolutions 1539 (2004) and 1612 (2005) to all conflict areas
of Afghanistan, in order to allow full monitoring of parties committing violations
against children and prompt advocacy and effective response to all violations and
abuses committed against children.
I also encourage you to strengthen the efforts of the United Nations country
team to bring the Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting to its full capacity in
order to ensure that accurate, objective, reliable and comprehensive information is
collected and communicated on the violations and abuses committed against
children.
I would like to request you to continue to allocate human and technical
resources to ensure that all children affected by conflict in all regions are covered
under the monitoring and reporting mechanism, as well as to explore ways to ensure
that the United Nations country team works in close consultation with the
Government of Afghanistan and the international military forces in order to support
their efforts to minimize civilian casualties, in line with international humanitarian
law.
I would ask you to address the importance of ensuring access for relevant
organizations, as applicable, to all prisons and places of detention in Afghanistan,
and ensuring that those organizations follow up, as appropriate, with the monitoring
and reporting mechanism with regard to children detained because of their alleged
association with armed groups; and to ensure as a matter of priority the continuous
implementation of the Security Council’s request for a child protection component
in the United Nations Assistance Mission in Afghanistan, in particular through the
appointment of child protection advisers in the Mission.
Furthermore, I would invite you to request the United Nations Development
Programme, UNICEF and other relevant United Nations entities, within their
respective mandates and in close cooperation with the Government of Afghanistan,
to continue to address socio-economic issues, which will also contribute to
addressing the welfare of children affected by armed conflict, including
strengthening national institutions; and further to provide assistance in the
