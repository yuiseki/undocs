Symbols of United Nations documents are composed of capital letters combined with
figures. Mention of such a symbol indicates a reference to a United Nations document,
Documents of the Security Council (symbol S/. . .) are normally published in quarterly
Supplements of the Official Records of the Security Council. The date of the document
indicates the supplement in which it appears or in which information about it is given.
The resolutions of the Security Council, numbered in accordance with a system
adopted in 1964, are published in yearly volumes of Resolutions and Decisions of the
Securiv Council, The new system, which has been applied retroactively to resolutions

SIXTEEN HUNDRED AND TWENTIETH MEETING
Held in New York on Tuesday, 21 December 1971, at 4 p.m.
President: Mr. I. B. TAYLOR-KAMARA

(Sierra Leone).

Present: The representatives of the following States: Argentina, Belgium, Burundi,
China, France, Italy, Japan, Nicaragua, Poland, Sierra Leone, Somalia, Syrian Arab
Republic, Union of Soviet Socialist Republics, United Kingdom of Great Britain and
Northern Ireland and United States of America.
Official communiqd
In accordance with rule 55 of the provisiona rules of procedure of the Security
Council, the following communique was issued by the Secretary-General and is circulated
in place of a verbatim record:
“At its 1620th meeting, held in private on 21 December 1971, the Security
Council continued its consideration of the question of the recommendation for the
appointment of the Secretary-General of the United Nations.
“Having received a number of nominations for the post of Secretary-General, the
Council voted by secret ballot on those nominations. As a result of the balloting, the
Security Council proceeded to adopt unanimously the following resolution:
” ‘The Security Council,
“ ‘Naving considered the question of the recommendation for the appointment of
the Secretary-General of the United Nations,
“ ‘Recommends to the General Assembly that Mr. Kurt Waldheim ,be appointed
Secretary-General of the United Nations.’ ”
