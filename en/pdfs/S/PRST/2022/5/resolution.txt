Statement by the President of the Security Council
At the 9090th meeting of the Security Council, held on 12 July 2022, in
connection with the Council’s consideration of the item entitled “United Nations
peacekeeping operations”, the President of the Security Council made the following
statement on behalf of the Council:
“The Security Council reaffirms its primary responsibility for the
maintenance of international peace and security under the Charter of the United
Nations and further reaffirms its previous relevant resolutions and presidential
statements addressing peacekeeping.
“The Security Council underscores the importance of peacekeepin g as one
of the most effective tools available to the United Nations in the promotion and
maintenance of international peace and security, and reaffirms the basic
principles of peacekeeping, such as consent of the parties, impartiality, and
non-use of force, except in self-defence and defence of the mandate.
“The Security Council reaffirms that lasting peace is neither achieved nor
sustained by military and technical engagements alone, but through political
solutions and a comprehensive approach to sustaining peace, and stresses that
the pursuit of sustainable political solutions should guide the design and
deployment of United Nations peacekeeping operations.
“The Security Council notes the progress made in efforts undertaken by
the Secretary-General to mobilize all partners and stakeholders in support of
more effective United Nations peacekeeping through his initiatives “Action for
Peacekeeping” and “Action for Peacekeeping +”, which highlights the
importance of, inter alia, strategic communications in United Nations
peacekeeping operations.
“The Security Council underscores the importance of strategic
communications to the implementation of United Nations peacekeeping
operations’ mandates and to the safety and security of peacekeepers. The
Security Council recognizes that strategic communications and the provision of
accurate content, including in local languages and in coordination with national
authorities, as appropriate, by United Nations peacekeeping operations help
strengthen understanding of their mandates, manage expectations and garner
trust and support among relevant stakeholders, notably host governments and
local communities, and contribute to countering disinformation and
misinformation in order to strengthen United Nations peacekeeping operations’
ability to implement their mandates and enhance the safety and security of
peacekeepers.

“The Security Council recognizes that strategic communications play an
enabler and multiplier role across all mandated areas of United Nations
peacekeeping operations, including by helping calibrate messaging in response
to information gathered by peacekeeping operations, within the limits of their
mandate and area of operation. The Security Council welcomes ongoing efforts
by the Secretary-General to take into account strategic communications in
planning and decision-making of peacekeeping operations, and notes that a
whole-of-mission approach to strategic communications facilitates confidence building with local communities, as well as coordination betwe en military,
police and civilian components and with United Nations country teams.
Accordingly, the Security Council stresses the need to improve the culture of
strategic communications across civilian, military and police components of
United Nations peacekeeping operations and highlights the critical role played
by mission leadership in this regard.
“The Security Council underlines that strategic communications are an
important element of a comprehensive, integrated and effective approach to the
protection of civilians, and calls on United Nations peacekeeping operations in
the implementation of protection of civilians mandates to enhance community
engagement and awareness about the protection needs and capacities of local
communities, particularly through the use of strategic communications and
community engagement activities. The Security Council reaffirms the
importance of multilingualism from the planning stage, as appropriate, when
developing communications strategies. The Security Council underlines that
strategic communications can be an important tool to prevent conflict -related
sexual violence and encourages United Nations peacekeeping operations to
invest from the outset in dialogue and engagement with local actors, particularly
women and youth, in order to build from the bottom up a protective environment
for civilians.
“The Security Council reaffirms the importance of the full, equal and
meaningful participation of women in all stages of peace and political processes
and in United Nations peacekeeping operations at all levels and all positions,
including senior leadership positions, and encourages the Secretary-General and
United Nations peacekeeping operations to continue making use of strategic
communications and advocacy to advance the women, peace and security
agenda. The Security Council remains committed to increasing the number of
civilians and uniformed women in peacekeeping at all levels and in key
positions and encourages efforts in this regard.
“The Security Council reaffirms its support for the United Nations zerotolerance policy on all forms of sexual exploitation and abuse, welcomes the
Secretary-General`s continued efforts to implement it and recognizes the
importance of communication of this policy among all personnel and rel evant
stakeholders, including troop- and police-contributing countries and local
communities.
“The Security Council emphasizes the importance of enhancing the safety
and security of peacekeepers, and notes with great concern the increasing
amount of disinformation and misinformation directed against United Nations
peacekeeping operations, which may negatively impact missions and
peacekeepers. The Security Council stresses the need for an integrated approach
to address this challenge in peacekeeping settings and welcomes ongoing efforts
by the Secretary-General in this regard. The Security Council reaffirms the
critical link between safety and security and the performance of civilian and
uniformed peacekeeping personnel, and that institutionalizing a cultu re of
performance in United Nations peacekeeping will contribute to better delivery

of peacekeeping mandates, as well as lead to improvements in the safety and
security of peacekeepers.
“The Security Council underscores that the primary responsibility fo r the
safety and security of United Nations personnel and assets rests with the host
state and highlights the importance of effective communications between United
Nations peacekeeping operations and host governments to build trust and mutual
understanding, thereby improving the safety and security of peacekeepers. The
Security Council reaffirms its call on all Member States hosting peacekeeping
operations to promptly investigate and effectively prosecute those responsible
for attacks on United Nations personnel and assets, with a view to prevent
impunity and discourage violence against peacekeepers, in line with resolution
2589 (2021), and to keep the concerned troop- and police-contributing countries
informed on the progress of such investigations and prosecutions in line with
resolution 2518 (2020).
“The Security Council takes note of the development of a strategy for the
digital transformation of United Nations peacekeeping, recognizes the
importance of leveraging existing communication technologies with a view to
support mandate delivery and enhance the safety and security of peacekeepers,
and, in this context, welcomes ongoing initiatives, suc h as the roll out of Unite
Aware. The Security Council encourages peacekeeping operations and relevant
stakeholders, including troop- and police-contributing countries, to support and
make full use, as appropriate, of available field-focused, reliable and costeffective communication technologies, in support of the delivery of
peacekeeping mandates and in accordance with the purposes and principles of
the Charter of the United Nations and the basic principles of peacekeeping, and
stresses the need for consultations with Member States and host countries, as
appropriate.
“The Security Council reiterates its call upon the Secretary-General to
continue to review and ensure uniformity of United Nations standards on
training and performance and welcomes the initiative by the Secretary-General
to update the 2017 Policy on Strategic Communications and Public Information.
The Security Council encourages the Secretary-General to continue to develop,
in close consultation with relevant stakeholders, including troop - and policecontributing countries, a strategy on strategic communications, as well as
specialized policies, guidelines and training materials on the topic, which should
be implemented across all components of peacekeeping operations, with the
objective of integrating strategic communications in planning, decision-making
and implementation of relevant activities.
“The Security Council recognizes the need to continue to improve
peacekeeping operations’ strategic communications capabilities, and
encourages the United Nations Secretariat and Member States, including troop and police-contributing countries, to work together in this regard. The Security
Council recognizes that effective strategic communica tions rely heavily on the
adequate, context-specific training of civilian and uniformed personnel alike
and highlights the importance of recruiting, as appropriate, qualified Military
Public Information Officers and Police strategic communications/public
information focal points with relevant experience across United Nations
peacekeeping operations.
“The Security Council highlights that, in transition settings, a proactive
approach to strategic communications by United Nations peacekeeping
operations may contribute to creating conditions conducive to the smooth
reconfiguration of United Nations presence, and recognises that the transition
