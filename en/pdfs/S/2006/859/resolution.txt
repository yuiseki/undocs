Letter dated 1 November 2006 from the Secretary-General
addressed to the President of the Security Council
I refer to the letter of your predecessor dated 17 October 2006 (S/2006/819), in
which he informed me that the members of the Security Council had taken note of
my intention expressed in my letter of 28 September 2006 (S/2006/778) to continue
the activities of the United Nations support team to the Cameroon-Nigeria Mixed
Commission with funding from the regular budget.
As requested by the members of the Council, I attach further information and
clarification on the activities expected to be performed by the Mixed Commission in
2007 and following years and on those activities to be performed by the United
Nations team (see annex).
I should be grateful if you would bring the present letter and its annex to the
attention of the members of the Security Council.
(Signed) Kofi A. Annan

Annex
Cameroon-Nigeria Mixed Commission: Coming activities
to be performed
1.
The Cameroon-Nigeria Mixed-Commission was established to follow up the
implementation of the 10 October 2002 ruling of the International Court of Justice
on the territorial dispute between the two countries. The Mixed Commission is
composed of Cameroon, Nigeria, and the United Nations. It is chaired by A. OuldAbdallah, Special Representative of the Secretary-General for West Africa. The
United Nations team is entrusted with the implementation of the Mixed
Commission’s mandate which covers the following areas:
• Technical and substantive support in political, legal and economic affairs,
including for the preparation of meetings and field visits
• Field support for the demarcation of the land boundary between the two
countries — pertaining to cartographic work, surveying and field
assessments — and oversight of the implementation of projects by external
contractors, including the pillar emplacement (as financed through voluntary
contributions)
• Monitoring the situation in areas of withdrawal and transfer of authority (Lake
Chad area, along the land boundary and the Bakassi peninsula)
• Advising on confidence-building measures, including the reactivation of the
Lake Chad Basin Commission.
2.

Activities scheduled for 2007 include:
• Regular meetings of the Mixed Commission and of its ad hoc working groups,
as well as of the Follow-up Committee of the Greentree Agreement
• Continuing the demarcation exercise along the 1,700 km land boundary (this
border is longer than the combined borders of Timor-Leste/Indonesia,
Iraq/Kuwait and Eritrea/Ethiopia under the United Nations demarcation
processes). A total of 460 km have already been demarcated and around
750 km are expected to be demarcated by 2007
• Carrying out activities related to the civilian observation following the
peaceful and orderly transfer of authority in some 40 villages in the Lake Chad
area and along the land boundary and in the Bakassi peninsula
• Addressing issues related to the needs of the affected populations in border
areas
• Reporting on the application, by both Cameroon and Nigeria, of relevant
international instruments to ensure respect for the rights of the affected
populations
• Making recommendations on such confidence-building measures as the
development of projects to promote joint economic ventures and cross-border
cooperation
• Supporting the meetings of the Lake Chad Basin Commission scheduled for

• Sustaining the efforts of the African Development Bank towards progress in
the multi-donor project for the Enugu (Nigeria) — Mutengene (Cameroon)
cross-border road
• Completing outstanding issues, further to the delineation of the maritime

• Contributing to an agreement on the tri-point between Cameroon, Nigeria and
Equatorial Guinea
• Seeking further voluntary contributions as urged by the Security Council and
the General Assembly.
3.

Activities to take place after 2007 include:
• Continuing the demarcation on the remaining segments of the land boundary,
which is estimated to be completed by 2008
• Monitoring and supporting the obligations of the parties in the Bakassi
peninsula in conformity with the Greentree Agreement, which defines a zone
to remain under Nigerian administration until 2008
• Following up the rights of affected populations in border areas, based on the
progress in the demarcation exercise along the land boundary, and according to
the Greentree Agreement on the Bakassi peninsula
• Continuing monitoring progress in joint economic ventures and cross-border
cooperation activities
• Following up the activities of both the Lake Chad Basin Commission and the
Gulf of Guinea Cooperation Council.
