Letter dated 20 August 2015 from the Secretary-General
addressed to the President of the Security Council
Further to my letter to the President of the Security Council of 26 June 2015
(S/2015/494) and my report on the United Nations operation in Cyprus of 2 July
2015 (S/2015/517), I have the honour to write to you on the subject of confidence building measures and the supporting role played by the United Nations
Peacekeeping Force in Cyprus (UNFICYP), both of which the Security Council
welcomed in its resolution 2234 (2015).
As you are aware, on 28 May 2015, the Greek Cypriot and Turkish Cypriot
leaders announced further confidence-building measures that would bring the two
communities closer together, including the opening of additional crossing points,
beginning at Lefka-Aplici/Lefke-Aplıç and Deryneia/Derynia. The Security Council,
in its deliberations of 22 July 2015, strongly welcomed that initiative.
Actively seeking to support those agreements, the United Nations Mine Action
Service, facilitated by UNFICYP, has conducted risk assessments around the
proposed crossing points in the buffer zone. To move forward with regard to the two
crossings and ensure the safety of Cypriots, as well as the UNFICYP personnel who
will patrol and monitor the area, mine clearance will be required in those areas,
north of the Turkish forces ceasefire line.
Pursuant to my above-mentioned letter to the President of the Security Council
and his letter of reply of 30 June 2015 (S/2015/495), a demining team is currently
deployed from the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) to UNFICYP
under the framework of inter-mission cooperation to clear a hazardous area within
the buffer zone, where heavy rains washed down mines from a Turkish f orces
minefield. To date, nearly two thirds of the area has been cleared.
Mindful of the importance of an early realization of the agreement by the
leaders in lending further momentum to the settlement talks, I should be grateful for
the concurrence of the Security Council for the UNIFIL demining team to remain in
Cyprus under the current arrangement until December 2015 to complete the current
undertaking and the proposed clearance at the above-mentioned crossings.
I should be grateful if you would bring this letter to the attention of the
members of the Security Council.
(Signed) BAN Ki-moon
