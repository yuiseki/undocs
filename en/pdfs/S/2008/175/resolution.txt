Letter dated 11 March 2008 from the Secretary-General addressed
to the President of the Security Council
I refer to the letter dated 29 February 2008 (S/2008/140) from the President of
the Security Council requesting a comprehensive status report on the processing of
letters of credit pertaining to the oil-for-food programme.
The Council will recall that at the meeting of the Working Group of
representatives of the Government of Iraq, the Central Bank of Iraq and the
Secretariat, held in Amman from 7 to 17 January 2008, it was reported that there
were 210 outstanding letters of credit, as at 31 December 2007 (see S/2008/41). As
shown in the attached note, I am pleased to report that the number of outstanding
letters of credit has since decreased to 167. This number, however, still remains
high. Moreover, I wish to inform the Council that since I last reported to the Council
in January, the processing of the authentication documents by the Government of
Iraq has remained slow. Given the uncertainty as to what progress will have been
made by the end of March, I would recommend that the situation be reviewed by the
Working Group early in May on the basis of results achieved as at 30 April 2008. If
the Council agrees, I shall provide a further progress report in May, after a followup meeting of the Working Group.
In the light of the recent developments, an initial amount of $100 million of
the $225 million held pending resolution of certain issues could be released and
transferred to the Development Fund for Iraq pursuant to Security Council
resolution 1483 (2003), subject to no objection by the Council. With respect to the
unencumbered funds in the United Nations Iraq Account, I would recommend that
the $187 million held as a financial reserve continue to be held.
(Signed) Ban Ki-moon

I.

Status of the United Nations Iraq Account
1.
As at 29 February 2008, the United Nations Iraq Account contained the United
States dollar equivalent amount of approximately $1.023 billion, which is composed
of €526 million in the euro sub-account with United States dollar equivalent of
799 million (at a rate of 1.5128) and $228 million in the United States dollar
account of the United Nations Iraq Account. Of this total amount, approximately
$605 million is held in the cash collateral portion of the account to cover expired
letters of credit for which claims of delivery have been made. The remaining amount
of approximately $418 million is currently held in the non-collateral portion of the
account and corresponds to the following amounts: (a) $187 million which is being
held in the account as a reserve for any unanticipated claims until such issues are
resolved pursuant to paragraph 16 of the note attached to my letter to the President
of the Security Council dated 10 July 2006 (S/2006/510); (b) $225 million originally
held in the account as collateral in respect of expired letters of credit, which funds
have not been transferred to the Development Fund for Iraq pending the resolution
of claims of delivery from suppliers in those cases where the Government of Iraq
has not issued authentication documents pursuant to the notes attached to my letters
to the President of the Security Council dated 25 July 2007 (S/2007/476),
7 December 2007 (S/2007/725) and 23 January 2008 (S/2008/41); and (c) any
unrealized gains from the strengthening of the euro against the United States dollar.

II.

Status of the letters of credit as at 29 February 2008
2.
As at 29 February 2008, there were 167 outstanding letters of credit (see table 1
and annexes I, II and III for details) representing an aggregate amount of
$605 million.
3.
It should be noted that the number of letters of credit decreased from 210 at
the end of December 2007 to 167 at the end of February 2008. This decrease is
mainly due to the letters of credit that were cancelled in January and February 2008
(see annex IV for details). It should also be noted that the number of letters of credit
with claims of delivery has decreased from 164 (with a value of approximately
$175 million of claims of delivery) at the end of December 2007 to 148 (with a
value of approximately $264 million of claims of delivery) at the end of February
2008 (see tables 1.A and 1.B). The increase in the value of the claims is due in part
to the new shipments made in December 2007 and the presentation of new
documents to BNP Paribas by the beneficiaries of the letters of credit and in part to
the strengthening of the euro in relation to the dollar for those letters of credit
denominated in euros.
4.
Despite the decrease in the number of letters of credit the processing of the
confirmations of arrival by the Government of Iraq has been slow in January and
February 2008. The commitment entered into by the concerned ministries at a

III.

Claims of delivery by suppliers and status of the Working
Group meeting
7.
As indicated in the letter dated 8 November 2007 from the President of the
Security Council to the Secretary-General, the members of the Council expressed
their ongoing concern with respect to the slow and non-provision of confirmations
of arrival by the Government of Iraq and were also concerned that there were
expired letters of credit for which there were claims of delivery from the suppliers.

4

S/2008/175

8.
Notwithstanding the continued calls from the Security Council and the
Secretariat, the issuance of confirmations of arrival has been very slow and at times
not forthcoming. Under existing procedures pursuant to Security Council resolution
986 (1995), payments cannot be made to suppliers who have delivered goods and
services in Iraq without the receipt of the corresponding confirmations of arrival
from the Central Bank of Iraq.
9.
As a result of the meeting of the Working Group, the Government of Iraq had
undertaken to take certain steps to resolve the claims of delivery for which
shipments had been made on or before 31 December 2007. In order to terminate
efficiently the operations of the letters of credit under the oil-for-food programme,
the Working Group had agreed that the concerned ministries of Iraq will confirm
delivery and issue and transmit the necessary confirmations of arrival to the Central
Bank of Iraq following the session of the Working Group. If the confirmations of
arrival were delivered to the Secretariat before 31 March 2008, payment under the
respective letter of credit would be made by BNP Paribas.
10. Notwithstanding these efforts, the pace of the delivery of the confirmations of
arrival from the Central Bank of Iraq to the United Nations Secretariat has not
improved, as a result of which there are currently 148 letters of credit (see table 3)
with an approximate value of $264 million where the letters of credit have expired
on or before 31 December 2007 but for which there are claims of delivery from the
suppliers based upon documents pending with BNP Paribas.
11. The status of the letters of credit as at 29 February 2008 and updated
information after the Working Group meeting in January 2008 is shown in tables 3
and 4.

12. It should be recalled that 17 letters of credit with claims of delivery were
referred to in my letter to the President of the Security Council dated 25 July 2007
(S/2007/476). The Security Council requested the Secretariat to issue all necessary
documentation to BNP Paribas for early settlement of the related payments
associated with those letters of credit in the letter from the President of the Security
Council dated 8 November 2007. As requested, the Secretariat had issued all the
necessary documentation to BNP Paribas for the settlement of the 17 letters of credit
referred to above. To date, BNP Paribas has paid the beneficiaries of nine letters of
credit including those related to communication numbers 600882 (paid on
27 December 2007), 631025 (paid on 13 December 2007), 802679 (paid on
7 January 2008), 900032 (paid on 7 March 2008), 900045 (paid on 8 January 2008),
1000623 (paid on 17 December 2007), 1001817 (paid on 17 December 2007),
1100100 (paid on 8 January 2008) and 1300211 (paid on 28 December 2007). The
payments related to eight letters of credit including communication numbers
601441, 601909, 800784, 802945, 901306, 1001636, 1100171 and 1200538 are
currently being reviewed by the United Nations Secretariat or BNP Paribas for
payment processing.
13. In an effort to move towards the termination of the operations relating to the
letters of credit under the programme, the Secretariat has continued to regularly
cancel letters of credit that have expired on their own terms with no claim of
delivery asserted by the supplier. As a result of the activities of the Working Group,
there were 57 letters of credit that were targeted for cancellation, of which 39 were
cancelled subsequent to the meeting in Amman and one was paid in full (see annex
IV). There are 17 letters of credit remaining targeted for cancellation, which are
currently being evaluated by BNP and/or the United Nations Secretariat with a view
to determining whether they could be cancelled and whether the associated cash
collateral could be released from the collateral portion to the non-collateral portion
of the United Nations Iraq Account.
14. To maximize the number of letters of credit to be cancelled, the Secretariat
will continue its existing process of reviewing the expired letters of credit for which
no claim of delivery has been made to determine whether a letter of credit could be
cancelled and the associated cash collateral released from the collateral portion to
the non-collateral portion of the United Nations Iraq Account. Such funds would in
principle be available for transfer to the Development Fund for Iraq in accordance
with Security Council resolution 1483 (2003).
15. With regard to the unresolved issues, the Government of Iraq committed itself
to make every effort to settle outstanding issues in cooperation with the United
Nations Secretariat in the first quarter of 2008. However, in the past month virtually
no progress has been made in the delivery of the confirmations of arrival to the
Secretariat. Therefore, I would propose that the situation be reviewed by the
Working Group early in May on the basis of results achieved as at 30 April 2008.
This would be followed by a meeting of the Working Group early in May and a
report to the Council later that month.
16. I believe this would give time for the Government of Iraq and an opportunity
to make every effort to resolve all issues and to settle outstanding issues in
cooperation with the United Nations Secretariat.

IV.

Release of cash collateral from the United Nations Iraq Account
18. It should be recalled that in my letter of 10 July 2006 to the President of the
Security Council (S/2006/510), I indicated that, unless there was an objection from
the Council, funds in the amount of $187 million would be held in the United
Nations Iraq Account as a reserve for any unanticipated claims from suppliers. This
$187 million reserve should continue to be maintained in the account. It is also
recalled that in my letters of 25 July 2007 (S/2007/476) and 23 January 2008
(S/2008/41), I indicated that additional funds amounting to $225 million would be
retained pending resolution of the issue of claims of delivery from suppliers. The
Council duly took note of this position.
19. Given the limited progress made so far in liquidation of the programme
through the cancellation of certain letters of credit, the Secretary-General would
intend, subject to no objection by the Council, to transfer to the Development Fund
an initial tranche of $100 million of the $225 million of unencumbered funds held in
the account referred to above, consistent with the provisions of Security Council
resolution 1483 (2003).
