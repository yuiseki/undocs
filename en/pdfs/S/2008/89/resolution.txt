Enclosure
Monthly report to the United Nations on the operations of the
Kosovo Force
1.

This report covers the period from 1 to 31 December 2007.

2.
As at 31 December 2007, the total number of troops in theatre was 15,002.
This includes 2,357 troops from non-NATO countries.
Political situation
3.
The Troika completed its mandate on 10 December 2007 shortly after the
Contact Group submitted its report on the Troika-led status negotiation process to
the Secretary-General of the United Nations. Subsequently, the Security Council
discussed the report and the situation in Kosovo at a closed-door session on
19 December 2007.
4.
At their meeting in Brussels on 7 December 2007, NATO Foreign Ministers
agreed that troops of the Kosovo Force (KFOR) would remain in Kosovo on the
basis of Security Council resolution 1244 (1999), unless the Security Council
decided otherwise. They also renewed their commitment to maintain the national
force contributions to KFOR, including reserves, at current levels and with no new
caveats.
Security situation
5.
On 2 December 2007, an explosion was reported in a bar in Prizren in southwestern Kosovo, which injured seven people. The motive remains unclear.
Authorities believe rival gangs involved in human trafficking could be behind this
attack.
6.
On 6 December 2007, Multinational Task Force Centre and the Kosovo Police
Service (KPS) reported that a civilian bus with 40 passengers of different ethnicities
had been stopped by 10 masked and armed men on the administrative boundary line.
No one was harmed and the bus eventually continued on its way under police escort.
7.
On 10 December 2007, a student demonstration sponsored by the Albanian
Student Union group took place in downtown Pristina. There were approximately
1,800 to 2,000 participants. No incidents were reported.
8.
On 18 December 2007, a Kosovo Serb demonstration took place in Mitrovica
North (Multinational Task Force North). The demonstration, organized by the
Serbian National Council of Northern Kosovo, was attended by approximately 2,500
participants. No incidents were reported.
9.
On 21 December 2007, the former Prime Minister of Kosovo, Ramush
Haradinal, and his associate Laji Brahimaj, were provisionally released by the
International Tribunal for the Former Yugoslavia to spend the holidays and New
Year in Kosovo until 7 January 2008.
10. During December 2007 no incidents against KFOR were recorded. The threat
level against KFOR remained low.
11. A total of 484 incidents related to drugs (5), counterfeit currency (54),
smuggling (24), explosions (6), miscellaneous criminal incidents (240), illegal
weapons possession (80), weapons and ammunition seizures (42) and unexploded

KFOR operations
12. The overall situation in Kosovo remained calm but tense throughout December
2007. All multinational task forces continued to focus on interoperability and
intelligence gathering while conducting high-visibility operations throughout
Kosovo to deter any possible attempt at destabilization and to maintain the safe and
secure environment. The operational reserve force battalion, provided by Germany,
deployed for an additional operational rehearsal from 16 November to 16 December
2007. KFOR also conducted an operational rehearsal from 1 to 7 December 2007 for
the 2008 operational reserve force battalions from Germany, the United Kingdom
and Italy and for the European Union Force tactical reserves. The Commander of
KFOR updated his guidance to his subordinate commanders in December, directing
them to be able to react quickly, to maintain mobility and flexibility, to ensure
KFOR freedom of action, to coordinate with UNMIK police and the Kosovo Police
Service, and to stress the importance of the reserves.
Compliance
13. During the month of December, there was one session of the disciplinary board
of the Kosovo Protection Corps (KPC) which resulted in the dismissal of four KPC
members. Two of these members are from minority communities.
Kosovo Protection Corps
14.

There was no recruitment campaign in December 2007.

15. The current active personnel establishment is 2,908. There are 193 (6.63 per
cent) active ethnic minority members (169 male, 24 female). The total number of
ethnic minorities remains the same compared to last month. The percentage of
minorities within KPC remains short of the goal established by the Special
Representative of the Secretary-General and there are 113 open posts left in the
active personnel establishment list. The reserve list includes 2,000 positions, with
1,892 filled (including 48 ethnic minority members, which equates to 2.45 per cent).
16. Training of the KPC members continues according to plan. A joint KPC/KFOR
senior leader seminar on the subject of strategic communications was conducted at
KPC headquarters on 26 December 2007 with 22 participants and at the training and
doctrine centre on 27 December 2007 with 20 LTC and MAJ participants. English
language courses continued at the centre with 150 KPC participants.
17. During the month of December, 13 ceremonies involving 387 KPC members
took place. No incidents were reported.
Conclusion
18. The situation remained calm and stable despite continued political uncertainty
related to Kosovo’s future status. Planning for the post-resettlement period
continues. KFOR remains fully prepared to deal with unrest and would respond
adequately to any attempt to disrupt the safe and secure environment, regardless of
where it comes from.
