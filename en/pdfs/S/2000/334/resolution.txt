Letter dated 18 April 2000 from the Secretary-General addressed
to the President of the Security Council
I have the honour to refer to paragraph 17 of Security Council resolution 1291
(2000) of 24 February 2000 concerning the expansion of the United Nations
Organization Mission in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (MONUC). In that
paragraph, the Council expressed serious concern at reports of the illegal
exploitation of natural resources and other forms of wealth in the Democratic
Republic of the Congo, including in violation of the sovereignty of that country,
called for an end to such activities, expressed its intention to consider the matter
further and requested me to report to the Council within 90 days on ways to achieve
this goal.
The Security Council may wish to consider creating a panel of experts, perhaps
for a six-month period, to undertake a preliminary investigation into the situation
and to revert to the Council thereafter with its recommendations. The panel would
be financed from the regular budget of the United Nations. It would thus be
organizationally separate from MONUC, although it could request logistical support
from the Mission while travelling within the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It
is envisaged that the panel would submit a preliminary report after three months and
a final report, with recommendations, at the end of its mandate.
The members of the panel should be selected on the basis of their expertise in
matters connected with natural resources and their knowledge of the subregion and
the political, military and economic situation there. I would propose that the panel
be based at the United Nations Office in Nairobi.
I should be grateful if you would bring this matter to the attention of the
members of the Security Council.
(Signed) Kofi A. Annan
