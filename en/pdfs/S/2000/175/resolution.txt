LETTER DATED 2 MARCH 2000 FROM THE SECRETARY-GENERAL
ADDRESSED TO THE PRESIDENT OF THE SECURITY COUNCIL

With reference to your letter dated 1 March 2000 (S/2000/167), I should
like to inform you that in a letter dated 1 March 2000 addressed to the
Permanent Representative of Iraq to the United Nations, Saeed Hasan, the
Executive Director of the Office of the Iraq Programme informed him of the
Security Council’s decision to authorize the Secretary-General to proceed with
the necessary arrangements regarding the Hajj pilgrimage outlined in the
statement of implementation as proposed by the Secretary-General (S/2000/166,
annex).
In a letter dated 2 March 2000 to the Executive Director of the Office of
the Iraq Programme, the Permanent Representative of Iraq stated, inter alia,
that the response to the request made by the Government of Iraq had come "after
a considerable delay and there [was] no longer any time to study any proposal".
Copies of the correspondence between the Executive Director of the Office of the
Iraq Programme and the Permanent Representative of Iraq are provided for your
information (see annexes I and II).

Letter dated 1 March 2000 from the Executive Director of the
Office of the Iraq Programme addressed to the Permanent
Representative of Iraq to the United Nations

I have the honour to refer to paragraph 26 of Security Council resolution
1284 (1999), in which the Council requested the Secretary-General to make the
necessary arrangements, for approval by the Council, to provide for reasonable
expenses related to the Hajj pilgrimage to be met by funds in the escrow account
established by its resolution 986 (1995). The Council also decided that Hajj
pilgrimage flights which do not transport cargo into or out of Iraq are exempt
from the provisions of paragraph 3 of Council resolution 661 (1990) and Council
resolution 670 (1990), provided timely notification of each flight is made to
the Security Council Committee established by resolution 661 (1990).
In his letter dated 24 February 2000 to the President of the Security
Council (S/2000/166), the Secretary-General, pursuant to paragraph 26 of Council
resolution 1284 (1999), and taking into full account the provisions of relevant
Security Council resolutions, in particular resolutions 661 (1990), 670 (1990)
and 986 (1995), submitted for the consideration of the Council the arrangements
to provide for reasonable expenses related to the Hajj pilgrimage to be met by
funds in the escrow account established by Council resolution 986 (1995). The
proposed arrangements were discussed by the Council at its informal
consultations held on 28 and 29 February 2000.
In a letter dated 1 March 2000 to the Secretary-General (S/2000/167), the
President of the Security Council informed the Secretary-General of the
Council’s decision to authorize him to proceed with the necessary arrangements
regarding the Hajj pilgrimage outlined in the statement of implementation as
proposed by the Secretary-General (S/2000/166, annex). As authorized by the
Council, the total amount of $2,000 per pilgrim, as identified by the Government
of Iraq, will be distributed at the headquarters of the United Nations Office of
the Humanitarian Coordinator for Iraq (UNOHCI) in Baghdad. Of this amount, $250
in cash and $1,750 in traveller’s cheques will be distributed to each pilgrim.
The distribution will be undertaken in the presence of UNOHCI personnel and
representatives of the Government of Iraq.
Recognizing the important religious duty of Iraqi Muslims to perform the
Hajj pilgrimage and noting the urgent time-frame involved, I should kindly like
to request the cooperation of the Government of Iraq in facilitating the
necessary arrangements. In this regard, I should like to request that the list
of the names of the Iraqi pilgrims be submitted to the United Nations, as
indicated in the letter of the Secretary-General dated 24 February 2000, and
would be grateful for the urgent delivery of this list to UNOHCI headquarters in
Baghdad by Saturday, 4 March 2000.
The total funds will be transferred to an established bank in Amman,
Jordan, where they will be issued appropriately and escorted by United Nations

Annex II
[Original:

Arabic]

Letter dated 2 March 2000 from the Permanent Representative of
Iraq to the United Nations addressed to the Executive Director
of the Office of the Iraq Programme

I have today received your letter dated 1 March 2000 containing your
proposal concerning the Hajj pilgrimage. I should like to inform you of our
position, as follows:

1.
It is deplorable that the members of the Security Council have been
discussing the question of the pilgrimage by Iraqi Muslims to the Holy City of
God for four months now. Since the beginning, we have asked, as is made clear
in our letters over the last three years, that payments for Iraqi pilgrims
should come out of Iraqi finances, and be made through their national Government
and financial institutions and not by any other means or from any other quarter.
The money in question is Iraqi money, not a gift from the Security Council or
the United States of America. From the beginning, Iraq put forward a proposal
that guarantees the safe distribution of Iraqi money to Iraqi pilgrims, as
confirmed in our most recent letter dated 2 February 2000 addressed to the
Chairman of the 661 Committee. However, the United States of America and the
United Kingdom have persistently blocked this proposal for political reasons. It
is regrettable that the issue of the pilgrimage, which is a religious and
humanitarian matter affecting millions of Muslims, has been turned into a
bargaining chip and a means by which the United States of America seeks to apply
political pressure. The United States of America, in its ignorance, believes
that it can mask its real intentions towards the Iraqi people. Everyone knows
that the United States of America and its British ally are the ones who are
insisting on the maintenance of the comprehensive and inhumane sanctions against
us, using the illegal aerial exclusion zones to attack our villages and towns on
a daily basis, and perpetrating all kinds of hostile acts in contravention of
the Charter of the United Nations and international law.
2.
Without going into details about the proposal which you mentioned in your
letter dated 1 March 2000 concerning the request that we have been making for
three years to the United Nations, and as reiterated in our recent letter dated
2 February 2000 addressed to the Chairman of the 661 Committee, while
recognizing the efforts made by the French and Russian representatives to
introduce improvements to the proposal of the Secretary-General of
24 February 2000, I regret to inform you that your response to our letter came
after a considerable delay and there is no longer any time to study any
proposal. The pilgrimage season has in fact already begun and the few Iraqi
pilgrims with the material means to be able to perform this religious duty have
