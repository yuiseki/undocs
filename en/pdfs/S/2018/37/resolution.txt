Letter dated 11 January 2018 from the President of the
Security Council addressed to the Secretary-General
Further to the letter dated 13 November 2017 from the Permanent
Representative of Italy, Ambassador Sebastiano Cardi, I have the honour to inform
you that the members of the Security Council have agreed to send a mission to
Afghanistan from 12 to 15 January 2018. They have also agreed on the terms of
reference of the mission (see annex).
I have the honour to lead this mission in my capacities as Permanent
Representative of Kazakhstan to the United Nations and President of the Security
Council for the month of January 2018.
Following consultations with the members, it has been agreed that the
composition of the mission will be as follows:
Ambassador Sacha Sergio Llorentty Solíz (Bolivia (Plurinational State of))
Mr. Bo Shen (China)
Ambassador Bernard Tanoh-Boutchoue (Côte d’Ivoire)
Ambassador Anatolio Ndong Mba (Equatorial Guinea)
Ambassador Tekeda Alemu (Ethiopia)
Ms. Anne Gueguen (France)
Ambassador Kairat Umarov (Kazakhstan)
Ambassador Mansour Alotaibi (Kuwait)
Ambassador Karel van Oosterom (Netherlands)
Ambassador Gustavo Meza-Cuadra (Peru)
Ambassador Joanna Wronecka (Poland)
Mr. Vladimir Safronkov (Russian Federation)
Ambassador Olof Skoog (Sweden)
Ambassador Jonathan Guy Allen (United Kingdom of Great Britain and
Northern Ireland)
Ambassador Nikki R. Haley (United States of America)
I should be grateful if you would have the present letter and its annex circulated
as a document of the Security Council.
(Signed) Kairat Umarov
President of the Security Council

Annex
Terms of reference of the Security Council mission to Afghanistan
I.

Context
1.
The Security Council will carry out its mission to Afghanistan from 12 to
15 January 2018 within the context of and in line with the following:
(a) The relevant statements by the President of the Security Council on the
situation in Afghanistan (S/PRST/2014/12 and S/PRST/2016/14);
(b)

Security Council resolution 2344 (2017) on the situation in Afghanistan;

(c) The most recent report of the Secretary-General on the situation in
Afghanistan and its implications for international peace and security ( S/2017/1056);
(d) The most recent report of the Secretary-General on the strategic review of
the United Nations Assistance Mission in Afghanistan (UNAMA) ( S/2017/696);
(e) The eighth report of the Analytical Support and Sanctions Monitoring
Team submitted pursuant to resolution 2255 (2015) concerning the Taliban and other
associated individuals and entities constituting a threat to the peace, stability and
security of Afghanistan (see S/2017/409).

II.

Objectives of the visit
2.
The members of the mission will meet with representatives of the Government
of Afghanistan, members of political parties and representatives of civil society and
of UNAMA and the Resolute Support Mission. The main objectives of the mission
are:
(a) To underscore that a conflict-free and violence-free Afghanistan is critical
for regional and global peace and security;
(b) To reiterate the support of the Security Council for the Government and
the people of Afghanistan and their efforts to rebuild their country, strengthen the
foundations of sustainable peace and constitutional democracy and assume thei r place
in the community of nations;
(c) To express strong support for UNAMA and its Head, the Special
Representative of the Secretary-General for Afghanistan, in fulfilling their mandates
as defined in resolution 2344 (2017);
(d) To review efforts by the Afghan authorities in support of an Afghan -led
development and stabilization process, with the assistance of the international
community, to address the instability caused by the Taliban, including the Haqqani
Network, as well as the threat to the security and stability of Afghanistan and the
region posed by Al-Qaida, ISIL (Da’esh) affiliates and other terrorist groups, violent
and extremist groups, illegal armed groups, criminals and those involved in t he
narcotics trade, trafficking in persons and the diversion of chemical precursors;
(e) To underscore the importance of continued progress on electoral reform
and towards the holding of credible and inclusive parliamentary elections in 2018 and
presidential elections in 2019;
(f) To underline the importance of an inclusive Afghan-led and Afghan-owned
peace process, in which women’s participation is promoted at all levels, for the longterm prosperity and stability of Afghanistan;

(g) To review the progress made by the Afghan National Unity Government,
with the assistance of the international community, including through capacity building initiatives, in addressing interconnected challenges, including in the areas of
security, economic and social development, governance, human rights and the rule of
law, women’s empowerment, regional cooperation and counter-narcotics;
(h) To encourage increased cooperation and coordination between Afghanistan
and the Central Asian States, including in intergovernmental relations and people -topeople contacts;
(i) To encourage increased cooperation and coordination among UNAMA,
the United Nations Regional Centre for Preventive Diplomacy for Central Asia and
relevant United Nations agencies and regional organizations to strengthen the
region’s capacity to overcome challenges to peace, stability and sustainable
development, including with respect to the areas of counter-terrorism and combating
cross-border organized crime, illicit drug trafficking, trafficking in persons, irregular
migration and the use of the Internet for criminal purposes;
(j) To recognize that development, peace and security and human rights are
interlinked and mutually reinforcing, to reaffir m the primary responsibility of the
Government and the authorities of Afghanistan in identifying, driving and directing
priorities, strategies and activities for sustaining peace, and in this regard, to
emphasize that inclusivity, including by ensuring the full and effective participation
of women, is key to advancing the peacebuilding process and objectives in
Afghanistan in order to ensure that the needs of all segments of society are taken into
account;
(k) To recognize the importance of linking security and development
objectives, in order to achieve durable peace and prosperity in Afghanistan and in the
wider region;
(l) To emphasize the importance of a regional approach to supporting security
and prosperity in and around Afghanistan, and to underlin e the commitment of the
Security Council to contributing constructively in this regard;
(m) To recommend a coordinated modus operandi for United Nations agencies
and programmes to increase the effectiveness of the assistance provided by the United
Nations on the ground, ensuring greater transparency in its administration, including
in the implementation of anti-corruption measures, as well a more effective use of
shrinking development and humanitarian aid, towards a harmonized United Nations
approach in Afghanistan and Central Asian countries.
