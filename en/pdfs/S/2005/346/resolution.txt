Letter dated 26 May 2005 from the Secretary-General addressed
to the President of the Security Council
I have the honour to refer to my letter dated 11 April 2005 (S/2005/236), by
which I forwarded to the Security Council, further to paragraph 1 (c) of article 13
ter of the Statute of the International Tribunal for the Prosecution of Persons
Responsible for Serious Violations of International Humanitarian Law Committed in
the Territory of the Former Yugoslavia since 1991 (the “International Tribunal”), the
22 nominations for ad litem judges of the Trial Chambers of that Tribunal which had
been received from Member States of the United Nations within the period specified
in paragraph 1 (b) of article 13 ter of the Statute as extended by the Security Council
in the decision taken at its 5140th meeting.
I also have the honour to refer to Security Council resolution 1597 (2005) of
20 April 2005, by which the Security Council amended article 13 ter of the
Tribunal’s Statute to allow for the re-election of existing ad litem judges and
decided to extend the deadline for nominations of ad litem judges under the
amended provision of the Statute for a further 30 days from the date of the adoption
of that resolution.
Pursuant to paragraph 1 (c) of article 13 ter of the Statute of the International
Tribunal as so amended, I have the honour to forward to the Security Council the
27 nominations for ad litem judges of the Trial Chambers of the International
Tribunal which have been received from Member States of the United Nations
within the period specified in paragraph (b) of article 13 ter of the Tribunal’s
Statute, as extended by the Security Council in resolution 1597 (2005). The list of
candidates,* in alphabetical order, is annexed to the present letter, together with
their curricula vitae, which have been provided in connection with their
nominations.
In this connection, I would like to draw the attention of the Security Council to
paragraph 1 (c) of article 13 ter of the Statute, which provides in the relevant part as
follows: “From the nominations received, the Security Council shall establish a list
of not less than 54 candidates, taking due account of the adequate representation of
the principal legal systems of the world and bearing in mind the importance of
equitable geographical distribution”.
I would note that the list of 27 candidates is short of the minimum number of
54 which is required by paragraph 1 (c) of article 13 ter of the Statute of the
