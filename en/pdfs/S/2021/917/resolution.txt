Letter dated 22 October 2021 from the President of the
Security Council addressed to the Secretary-General
Further to the letter dated 29 July 2021 from Ambassador Nicolas de Rivière, I
have the honour to confirm that the members of the Security Council have agreed to
send a mission to Mali and the Niger from 23 to 25 October 2021. The members have
also agreed on the terms of reference of the mission (see annex).
The mission will be co-led by Ambassador Nicolas de Rivière (France),
Ambassador Martin Kimani (Kenya) and Ambassador Abdou Abarry (Niger).
Following consultations with the members, it has been agreed that the
composition of the mission will be as follows:
Deputy Permanent Representative Dai Bing (China)
Ambassador Sven Jürgenson (Estonia)
Ambassador Nicolas de Rivière (France)
Deputy Permanent Representative Ravindra Raguttahalli (India)
Ambassador Geraldine Byrne Nason (Ireland)
Ambassador Martin Kimani (Kenya)
Deputy Permanent Representative Juan Gómez Robledo Verduzco (Mexico)
Ambassador Abdou Abarry (Niger)
Ambassador Mona Juul (Norway)
Deputy Permanent Representative Anna Evstigneeva (Russian Federation)
Ambassador Inga Rhonda King (Saint Vincent and the Grenadines)
Ambassador Tarek Ladeb (Tunisia)
Deputy Permanent Representative James Kariuki (United Kingdom)
Ambassador Linda Thomas-Greenfield (United States)
Ambassador Dinh Quy Dang (Viet Nam)
I should be grateful if you would have the present letter and its annex circulated
as a document of the Security Council.
(Signed) Martin Kimani
President of the Security Council

Annex
Terms of reference of the Security Council mission to Mali and the
Niger, 23 to 25 October 2021
1.
The Security Council will carry out a mission to Mali and the Niger from 23 to
25 October 2021 related to resolutions 2391 (2017) and 2584 (2021) and statement
by the President S/PRST/2021/16.
2.
The mission will visit Mali and the Niger, for meetings on the situation in Mali,
including on the efforts of the United Nations Multidimensional Integrated Stabilization
Mission in Mali (MINUSMA) to implement its mandate, and on the situation in the
Sahel, including on the efforts of the Group of Five for the Sahel (G5 Sahel).
3.

The mission will be co-led by Kenya, France and the Niger.

4.

The objectives of the mission are as follows:

(a) To assess the political, security, humanitarian and development efforts
carried out by the Transitional Government of Mali, and to assess the human rights
challenges in Mali and the mechanisms in place to monitor and address those;
(b) To assess the preparation for the holding of free and fair elections, within
the established 18-month timeline, in accordance with the Transition Charter,
including the presidential election on 27 February 2022, and the full realization of the
political transition in Mali;
(c) To assess the implementation by the Transitional Government of Mali and
the signatory armed groups of the priority measures of the Agreement on Peace and
Reconciliation in Mali referenced in paragraph 4 of resolution 2584 (2021) and to
enquire about steps envisaged by them for the fulfilment of those measures by the end
of the current mandate of MINUSMA;
(d) To assess the current situation in Central Mali, be informed of the measures
taken by the Malian authorities in implementing a comprehensive politically led
strategy to protect civilians, reduce intercommunal violence and re -establish State
presence, State authority and basic social services there, and to meet the priority
measures set out in paragraph 15 of resolution 2584 (2021);
(e) To assess the efforts of MINUSMA to implement its mandate, including
the standards referred to in paragraph 47 of resolution 2584 (2021), discuss the report
of the Secretary-General of 16 July on the progress of the force adaptation plan
implementation and recommendations on the force levels and ceiling of MINUSMA
uniformed personnel and assess the broader United Nations response to addressing
drivers of conflict and wider governance challenges in an integrated manner;
(f) To assess the level of operationalization of the Joint Forc e of the Group of
Five for the Sahel, including the implementation of the human rights compliance
framework referred to in resolution 2391 (2017) and the human rights due diligence
policy on United Nations support to non-United Nations security forces, as well as
the concurrent efforts implemented by the G5 Sahel in the political and development
fields, noting that the Secretary-General will have issued a report on the support to
the G5 Sahel joint force prior to the mission;
(g) To assess the level of coordination, exchange of information and, when
applicable, support, within their respective mandates and through existing
mechanisms, among MINUSMA, the Malian Defence and Security Forces, Joint
Force of the Group of Five for the Sahel, the French forces and the European Union
missions in Mali, as well as coordination with European partners, including Task
Force Takuba;

(h) To exchange views with the authorities in the Niger on the security,
development and humanitarian situation in the Sahel, including efforts to protect
refugees and internally displaced persons, the adverse effects of climate change on
the stability of the region and discuss the measures taken by the Government of the
Niger to address this situation;
(i) To reiterate the view of the Council that the stabilization of the situation
and the protection of civilians in the region are a primary responsibility of the
respective Governments and require a fully integrated response, led by the re spective
Governments, with the support of the United Nations Office for West Africa and the
Sahel, regional organizations and the international community, and to reiterate the
support of the Council to international and regional efforts aimed at stabilizin g the
Sahel region;
(j) To hear good practices in social cohesion in the Niger and discuss how
United Nations initiatives and mechanisms could help stem intercommunal violence
more tangibly in the region.
5.
The mission will seek to achieve these objectives through meetings with, among
others:
• The Transitional Government of Mali
• MINUSMA and other United Nations entities in Mali
• Actors involved in the implementation of the Agreement in Mali, including the
parties, and members of the international mediation
• The Economic Community of West African States and other representatives of
the mechanism established to ensure respect for the time -limited political
transition in Mali
• Representatives from the security presences mentioned in paragraph 32 of
resolution 2584 (2021)
• Members of civil society organisations, including women and youth
• The Government of the Niger
• The United Nations country team in the Niger
• The Executive Secretary of the G5 Sahel
• The Force Commander of the Joint Force of the Group of Five for the Sahel
6.
The mission may hold a commemorative ceremony in Mali to pay tribute to the
soldiers and the peacekeepers who lost their lives while serving in MIN USMA.
Another commemorative ceremony may also be held in the Niger to pay tribute to
civilian victims and soldiers who lost their lives combating terrorism.
7.
The mission will seek to implement lessons learned from previous Security
Council missions, including regarding modalities of association of representatives
from relevant regional organizations.
