Letter dated 4 October 2016 from the Secretary-General
addressed to the President of the Security Council
I wish to refer to Security Council resolution 2277 (2016), in which the
Council requested me to conduct a strategic review of the mandate of my Special
Envoy for the Great Lakes Region by 30 September 2016, in order to present
recommendations for the Special Envoy to better support Member States in the full
implementation of their commitments under the Peace, Security and Cooperation
Framework for the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the Region, in full
complementarity with the United Nations Organization Stabilization Mission in the
Democratic Republic of the Congo (MONUSCO) and taking into consideration the
evolving challenges facing the region.
From 14 to 24 June 2016, a strategic assessment mission that I had dispatched
conducted consultations in the region. While visiting Burundi, the Democratic
Republic of the Congo, Ethiopia and Kenya, the assessment team held discussions
with a wide range of stakeholders, including representatives of signato ry States and
guarantors of the Peace, Security and Cooperation Framework, bilateral and
multilateral donors and other partners. The review team held additional
consultations in New York with members of the Security Council and senior United
Nations officials.
The review team reported that the region faces significant challenges that
hinder the full implementation of the Peace, Security and Cooperation Framework.
These include difficulties and major delays in the neutralization of armed groups in
eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo; mistrust and limited cooperation
between Governments in the region; and dwindling interest in the implementation of
the Framework, owing in part to pressing domestic issues, notably elections.
Those political and security challenges have constrained the efforts of my
Special Envoy to implement the multifaceted mandate entrusted to him under
Security Council resolutions 2098 (2013), 2147 (2014), 2211 (2015) and 2277
(2016). Making recommendations to address all of the challenges was beyond the
scope of the review, which focused on how my Special Envoy could better assist
signatory States in implementing the Peace, Security and Cooperation Framework
and thus contribute to the consolidation of peace, security and cooperation in the
region.

All of the review team’s interlocutors continue to view the Peace, Security and
Cooperation Framework as an important political and diplomatic platform, bringing
together national Governments and regional organizations to address the challenges
confronting the Great Lakes region. They further believe that the mandate of my
Special Envoy remains adequate to tackle current and emerging peace and security
challenges in the region.
Following extensive consultations, the review team made the following
recommendations:
(a) My Special Envoy should dedicate most of his time and resources to
high-level political, diplomatic and strategic engagements on peace and security
issues;
(b) The Office of my Special Envoy should strengthen its partnership with
United Nations presences in the region, as well as with regional and subregional
organizations, through a carefully designed division of labour. Under the proposed
arrangement, my Special Envoy should guide United Nations agencies, funds and
programmes in carrying out activities that further the imple mentation of the Peace,
Security and Cooperation Framework, as envisaged in the Great Lakes Regional
Strategic Framework 2016-2017, which was endorsed by the Security Council in
March 2016;
(c) In cooperation with relevant United Nations entities, regiona l and
subregional organizations, donors and partners, my Special Envoy should intensify
his efforts to promote timely, credible and inclusive elections in the region, pursuant
to Security Council resolution 2211 (2015);
(d) Given that the mandate of my Special Envoy is currently articulated in
several pronouncements and decisions of the Security Council, for the sake of
clarity and focus, the Council may wish to adopt a consolidated resolution
encompassing the entire mandate of my Special Envoy;
(e) With the endorsement of the Security Council, my report to the Council
on the implementation of the Peace, Security and Cooperation Framework should be
retitled “Report of the Secretary-General on peace and security in the Great Lakes
region”. That recommendation is underpinned by the evolving peace and security
challenges facing the region, as well as the gradual expansion of the mandate of my
Special Envoy, since the Framework was signed in February 2013. With the
proposed new title, my future reports would address all peace and security issues in
the Great Lakes region, including issues that may not have been foreseen in the
Framework. The review team also suggested that the Security Council dedicate a
session to the Great Lakes region once a year, upon receiving my proposed report on
peace and security in the region in September. That session should be separate from
the consultations of the Council on MONUSCO;
(f) The Office of my Special Envoy should be provided with a lean internal
administrative capacity, with appropriate delegation of authority and sufficient
administrative flexibility to facilitate the implementation of his mandate;
(g) Given that the need for continued United Nations interaction with the
region, including through the International Conference on the Great Lakes Region,
extends beyond the scope of the Peace, Security and Cooperation Framework, it is

necessary to view the role of my Special Envoy as extending beyond the
Framework. That is especially important since many peace and security challenges
in the region predated the Framework and will persist in the foreseeable future;
(h) In view of the mandate of the Security Council and the extent of the

I intend to proceed with the implementation of the recommendations of the
strategic review that fall within my purview.
I should be grateful if you would bring the present letter to the attention of the
members of the Security Council.
(Signed) BAN Ki-moon
